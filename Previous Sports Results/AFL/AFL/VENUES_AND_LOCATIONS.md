# AFL Venues & Stadium Profiles — Historical & Modern

An exhaustive register of all major grounds, stadiums, and regional venues used by the VFL/AFL from 1900 through 2025.

---

## 1. Primary Modern AFL Stadiums

### Melbourne Cricket Ground (MCG) — Melbourne, Victoria
- **City / State:** Melbourne, Victoria, Australia
- **Opening Year:** 1853 (First VFL match: 1897)
- **Capacity:** 100,024 (Largest stadium in Southern Hemisphere)
- **Field Dimensions:** 160m length x 141m width (Curved oval)
- **Playing Surface:** Natural turf (hybrid reinforced couch/rye blend)
- **Tenant Clubs:** Richmond, Melbourne, Collingwood, Hawthorn, Carlton (marquee matches)
- **Orientation:** East-West alignment
- **Observations & Environmental Factors:**
  - True neutral Grand Final venue; expansive wings reward elite ball-movement, rebound transition, and structural width.
  - Wind patterns swirl off the massive four-tiered Great Southern Stand (Shane Warne Stand) and Olympic Stand.
  - Dew factors significantly in evening winter matches, depressing second-half mark-to-shot conversion.

### Marvel Stadium (Docklands Stadium) — Melbourne, Victoria
- **City / State:** Melbourne, Victoria, Australia
- **Opening Year:** 2000
- **Capacity:** 53,359
- **Field Dimensions:** 159.5m length x 128.5m width (Narrow corridor design)
- **Playing Surface:** Retractable roof; Eclipse Stabilised Hybrid Turf
- **Tenant Clubs:** St Kilda, Western Bulldogs, Essendon, North Melbourne, Carlton
- **Observations & Environmental Factors:**
  - Fast, enclosed track with roof closed (over 90% of fixtures); zero wind, temperature-controlled, elevated scoring shot accuracy.
  - Narrow wings funnel play aggressively through the corridor, generating high stoppage density and inside-50 tackle pressure.

### Adelaide Oval — Adelaide, South Australia
- **City / State:** Adelaide, South Australia, Australia
- **Redevelopment Year:** 2014 (First match 1870s)
- **Capacity:** 53,500
- **Field Dimensions:** 167m length x 124m width (Exceptionally long and narrow)
- **Tenant Clubs:** Adelaide Crows, Port Adelaide
- **Observations & Environmental Factors:**
  - North-South orientation creates severe swirling winds from the River Torrens end.
  - The narrow boundaries suppress boundary-line transition, heavily rewarding corridor kicks and elite intercept defenders.

### Optus Stadium (Perth Stadium) — Perth, Western Australia
- **City / State:** Perth, Western Australia, Australia
- **Opening Year:** 2018
- **Capacity:** 60,000
- **Field Dimensions:** 165m length x 130m width
- **Tenant Clubs:** West Coast Eagles, Fremantle Dockers
- **Observations & Environmental Factors:**
  - State-of-the-art fast hybrid surface; evening fixtures subject to the "Fremantle Doctor" sea breeze sweeping from the south-west.
  - High home-ground advantage due to interstate travel distance and hostile crowd acoustics.

### Sydney Cricket Ground (SCG) — Sydney, New South Wales
- **City / State:** Sydney, New South Wales, Australia
- **Opening Year:** 1880s
- **Capacity:** 48,000
- **Field Dimensions:** 155m length x 136m width (Shortest ground in modern AFL)
- **Tenant Clubs:** Sydney Swans
- **Observations & Environmental Factors:**
  - Short ground length reduces end-to-end transition time; clearances directly produce scoring shots.
  - Subtropical winter rain frequently creates heavy, contested tackling battles.

### The Gabba (Brisbane Cricket Ground) — Brisbane, Queensland
- **City / State:** Brisbane, Queensland, Australia
- **Capacity:** 36,000
- **Field Dimensions:** 156m length x 138m width
- **Tenant Clubs:** Brisbane Lions
- **Observations & Environmental Factors:**
  - High humidity in early rounds; true oval dimensions; slippery ball conditions in evening matches.

### GMHBA Stadium (Kardinia Park) — Geelong, Victoria
- **City / State:** Geelong, Victoria, Australia
- **Capacity:** 40,000 (following Stage 5 redevelopment)
- **Field Dimensions:** 170m length x 115m width (The longest and narrowest ground in professional football)
- **Tenant Clubs:** Geelong Cats
- **Observations & Environmental Factors:**
  - Extreme corridor constriction; requires unique boundary-pinning defensive structures. Strongest home-ground winning percentage in AFL history.

## 2. Historic & Regional AFL Venues

- **Waverley Park (VFL Park, Mulgrave):** 1970–1999. Massive 160,000 proposed, 78,000 realized capacity. Famed for icy Antarctic winds, freezing mud, and driving rain.
- **Princes Park (Optus Oval, Carlton):** Home of Carlton until 2005. Traditional suburban fortress.
- **Victoria Park (Abbotsford):** Collingwood fortress 1892–1999. Intimidating suburban boundary proximity.
- **Western Oval (Whitten Oval, Footscray):** Famed for extreme howling westerly winds off the Maribyrnong River.
- **Moorabbin Oval (St Kilda), Windy Hill (Essendon), Arden Street (North Melbourne), Glenferrie Oval (Hawthorn), Junction Oval (St Kilda/Fitzroy), Lake Oval (South Melbourne).**
- **Regional Venues:** Blundstone Arena (Hobart, extreme winds), University of Tasmania Stadium (Launceston), TIO Stadium (Darwin, wet season heat), Manuka Oval (Canberra, freezing winter nights), Norwood Oval (Gather Round), Adelaide Hills Summit Sport Park (Mount Barker).
