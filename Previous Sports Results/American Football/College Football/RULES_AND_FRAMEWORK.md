## 2. Core Playing Rules & Scoring Architecture

### Field Dimensions & Scrimmage Rules
- **Field Size:** 100 yards of playing field plus two 10-yard end zones (120 total yards length; 53 1/3 yards / 160 feet width).
- **Hash Marks:** 60 feet from each sideline (closer to center than NFL 70 feet 9 inches), creating distinct wide-side and short-side field geometry that drives collegiate play-calling.
- **Goalposts:** 18 feet 6 inches wide; crossbar 10 feet above ground level.

### Game Timing & Clock Rules
- **Regulation Playing Time:** Four 15-minute quarters (60 total minutes).
- **Halftime:** Standard 20 minutes (can be extended to 22–25 minutes for major bowls and CFP national championships).
- **Play Clock:** 40 seconds following play stoppage; 25 seconds following administrative stoppages (penalties, measurements, timeouts).
- **First Down Clock Rule (2023 Revision):** The clock continues running following a first down in the field of play, EXCEPT during the final two minutes of the second and fourth quarters.
- **Two-Minute Warning (2024 Revision):** An automatic administrative timeout occurs at 2:00 remaining in the second and fourth quarters.
- **In-Helmet Communications (2024 Revision):** One player on defense (green dot) and the quarterback may receive coach communication; audio shuts off with 15 seconds on the play clock or at the snap.

### Scoring Structure
| Scoring Event | Points Awarded | Governing NCAA Rule |
|---|---:|---|
| Touchdown (TD) | 6 Points | Rule 8, Section 2 |
| Point After Touchdown (PAT Kick) | 1 Point | Rule 8, Section 3 |
| Two-Point Scrimmage Conversion | 2 Points | Rule 8, Section 3 |
| Field Goal (FG) | 3 Points | Rule 8, Section 4 |
| Safety | 2 Points | Rule 8, Section 5 |
| Defensive Two-Point Conversion | 2 Points | Rule 8, Section 3 (Fumble/INT return on conversion attempt) |

## 3. Overtime Evolution & Resolution Procedures

Ties were eliminated from NCAA football beginning in the 1995 postseason and 1996 regular season. The overtime procedure has undergone three major safety and length-curtailing revisions:

### Historical Overtime Rules (1996–2018)
- Alternating possessions starting at the opponent's 25-yard line (no game clock; standard play clock applies).
- Coin toss decides who possesses first or which end of the field is used.
- Starting with the third overtime (3OT), teams scoring a touchdown were required to attempt a 2-point conversion.

### 2019–2020 Revision
- Starting with the fifth overtime (5OT), possessions from the 25-yard line were eliminated; teams ran alternating 2-point conversion plays from the 3-yard line.

### Current NCAA Overtime Rules (2021–Present)
1. **Period 1 (1OT):** Each team receives one possession starting at the opponent's 25-yard line. Teams may kick a PAT or attempt a 2-point conversion.
2. **Period 2 (2OT):** Teams alternate possessions from the 25-yard line. Teams scoring a touchdown **MUST attempt a 2-point conversion** (PAT kicks prohibited).
3. **Period 3+ (3OT and beyond):** Possessions from the 25-yard line are discontinued. Teams alternate **single 2-point conversion plays from the 3-yard line** in a sudden-death shootout until a winner emerges.

## 4. Postseason Championship Frameworks

```
1900–1935: Historic Selectors & Rose Bowl (Pre-Poll Era)
      │
1936–1991: AP Writers & UPI/Coaches Polls (Bowl Era)
      │
1992–1994: Bowl Coalition (Orange, Sugar, Cotton, Fiesta)
      │
1995–1997: Bowl Alliance (Fiesta, Orange, Sugar)
      │
1998–2013: Bowl Championship Series (BCS 1 vs. 2 Title Game)
      │
2014–2023: 4-Team College Football Playoff (CFP Semifinals & Championship)
      │
2024–Present: 12-Team Expanded College Football Playoff
```

### 12-Team CFP Tournament Structure (2024–Present)
- **Automatic Bids (5):** The five highest-ranked conference champions according to the CFP Selection Committee receive automatic tournament berths.
- **Top 4 First-Round Byes:** The four highest-ranked conference champions earn seeds 1 through 4 and receive first-round byes directly into the Quarterfinals.
- **At-Large Bids (7):** Seeds 5 through 12 are awarded to the seven next-highest ranked teams.
- **First Round:** Seeds 5–12 play on the campus home stadiums of seeds 5–8 (5 vs 12, 6 vs 11, 7 vs 10, 8 vs 9).
- **Quarterfinals & Semifinals:** Rotated among the New Year's Six bowl games (Rose, Sugar, Orange, Cotton, Fiesta, Peach).
- **National Championship Game:** Played at a bid-selected neutral NFL dome/stadium on the second or third Monday of January.

## 5. Officiating Corps & Neutrality Protocols

### On-Field Officiating Crew (8 Officials)
Modern college football utilizes an eight-person officiating crew:
1. **Referee (R):** Crew chief, positioned in offensive backfield; oversees quarterback protection, intentional grounding, and announces penalty enforcements.
2. **Umpire (U):** Positioned in the offensive backfield (or defensive linebackers pre-2010); monitors offensive line holding, illegal blocks, and interior line of scrimmage.
3. **Head Line-Judge / Down Judge (DJ):** Lines up on sideline; manages line of scrimmage, offsides, false starts, and chain crew.
4. **Line Judge (LJ):** Lines up opposite the Down Judge; monitors line of scrimmage, forward passes beyond scrimmage line, and sideline out-of-bounds.
5. **Field Judge (FJ):** Positioned 20–25 yards downfield along the sideline; monitors pass coverage, sideline catches, and field goal uprights.
6. **Side Judge (SJ):** Positioned 20–25 yards downfield opposite the Field Judge; monitors perimeter passing, defensive pass interference, and boundary spots.
7. **Back Judge (BJ):** Positioned in deep defensive secondary (25–30 yards); monitors deep pass coverage, safety play, 40/25 play clock, and field goal crossbar.
8. **Center Judge (CJ):** Positioned in the offensive backfield opposite the Referee; assists with ball spotting, pace of play, and line-of-scrimmage enforcement.

### Strict Conference Neutrality Mandate
In all postseason bowl games, CFP first-round games, quarterfinals, semifinals, and the CFP National Championship Game, **officiating crews are appointed strictly from conferences not represented in the game**. An SEC crew cannot officiate an SEC team; a Big Ten crew cannot officiate a Big Ten team. This ensures uncompromised impartiality.

### Instant Replay & Collaborative Replay Operations
- Replay officials in the stadium booth and centralized conference command centers review every play before the next snap.
- **Targeting (Rule 9-1-4):** Every targeting call is subject to mandatory video review. All four elements of targeting must be confirmed; if any element is lacking, the 15-yard penalty and player disqualification are overturned.

## 6. Analytical Modeling & Settlement Protocol

### Modeling Constraints & Market Governance
- **Base Rate Anchoring:** Models must anchor on college football scoring base rates (~54–58 total points in modern FBS; 4.0–4.4 yards per carry; 7.2–7.6 yards per pass attempt).
- **Layoff Impact Vectors:** Postseason bowl games involve extended layoff periods (3–5 weeks between regular season finale and bowl game); models must adjust team rust and tactical preparation weights accordingly.
- **Opt-Out & Transfer Portal Discounting:** In the modern transfer portal and NIL era, bowl models must dynamically adjust for starting quarterbacks, coordinators, or first-round NFL prospects opting out of non-playoff bowl games.
- **Neutral Venue Field Dynamics:** Neutral site championship games eliminate standard 2.5–3.0 point home-field advantage; models must evaluate crowd split distributions and stadium roof conditions (indoor dome vs open air).

### Settlement Lineage & Resolution Rules
- **Settlement Source:** Official NCAA Box Score and College Football Playoff Administrative Gamebook.
- **Official Game Threshold:** Matches must complete at least 55 minutes of play to be official for full-game betting and settlement under standard gaming rules.
- **Postponements & Weather Delays:** Uncompleted games suspended due to severe weather (lightning) that are resumed within 48 hours settle on the completed score; games cancelled permanently void all unresulted derivative markets.
