# College Football — Venues & Locations

An authoritative register of the iconic stadiums, bowl venues, and modern NFL super-stadiums that define American College Football history from 1900 to 2025.

---

## 1. Legendary Postseason Championship & Bowl Venues

### Rose Bowl Stadium (Pasadena, CA)
- **Nickname:** "The Granddaddy of Them All"
- **Opened:** 1922 | **Current Capacity:** 89,702 (formerly 104,000+)
- **Playing Surface:** Natural Grass (Tifway 419 Bermuda grass)
- **Historic Role:** Home of the Rose Bowl Game since 1923; hosted five Super Bowls; host of two BCS National Championship Games (2002, 2006) and the 2014 BCS Title Game; annual host of CFP Semifinals / Quarterfinals.
- **Architectural & Environmental Dynamic:** Open-air natural amphitheater located in the Arroyo Seco canyon with the San Gabriel Mountains backdrop; pristine natural grass surface that rewards speed and precision footing.

### Caesars Superdome / Louisiana Superdome (New Orleans, LA)
- **Opened:** 1975 | **Current Capacity:** 73,208 (expandable to 76,468)
- **Playing Surface:** Artificial Turf (FieldTurf Core) | **Structure:** Fixed Dome
- **Historic Role:** Home of the Sugar Bowl since 1975; host of four BCS National Championship Games (2000, 2004, 2008, 2012) and CFP National Championship Games (2020).
- **Environmental Dynamic:** Climate-controlled enclosed dome; notorious for extreme crowd noise levels exceeding 115 decibels during SEC and national championship clashes.

### Hard Rock Stadium & Miami Orange Bowl (Miami & Miami Gardens, FL)
- **Venues:**
  - **Miami Orange Bowl (Historic, 1937–2008):** Legendary home of the Orange Bowl; site of five national championship deciders (1983 Miami, 1993 FSU, 1994 Nebraska).
  - **Hard Rock Stadium (Modern, opened 1987):** Current home of the Orange Bowl, Miami Hurricanes, and CFP National Championship (2021, 2026). Capacity: 65,326.
- **Playing Surface:** Natural Grass (Tifway 419 Bermuda) with partial canopy roof covering spectator seating while leaving the playing field open to the elements.

### AT&T Stadium & Cotton Bowl Stadium (Dallas & Arlington, TX)
- **Venues:**
  - **Cotton Bowl Stadium (Fair Park, Dallas, opened 1930):** Historic host of the Cotton Bowl Classic (1937–2009) and the annual Texas vs. Oklahoma "Red River Rivalry."
  - **AT&T Stadium (Arlington, TX, opened 2009):** Capacity: 80,000 (expandable to 105,000); retractable roof with center-hung HD video board; host of the inaugural College Football Playoff National Championship Game (January 12, 2015).

### State Farm Stadium & Sun Devil Stadium (Tempe & Glendale, AZ)
- **Venues:**
  - **Sun Devil Stadium (Tempe, AZ, opened 1958):** Hosted the Fiesta Bowl (1971–2006); site of legendary 1987 Fiesta Bowl (Penn State 14, Miami 10) and 2003 Fiesta Bowl (Ohio State 31, Miami 24 in 2OT).
  - **State Farm Stadium (Glendale, AZ, opened 2006):** Retractable roof and rollout natural grass field; host of BCS title games (2007, 2011) and CFP title games (2016).

### Mercedes-Benz Stadium & Georgia Dome (Atlanta, GA)
- **Venues:**
  - **Georgia Dome (Historic, 1992–2017):** Hosted the Peach Bowl and SEC Championship Games.
  - **Mercedes-Benz Stadium (Modern, opened 2017):** Capacity: 71,000–78,000; features a retractable pinwheel roof and 360-degree halo video board; host of CFP National Championship Games (2018 Alabama vs. Georgia "2nd and 26", and 2025 Ohio State vs. Notre Dame).

### SoFi Stadium (Inglewood, CA)
- **Opened:** 2020 | **Capacity:** 70,240 (expandable to 100,240)
- **Playing Surface:** Matrix Turf with Organic Infill | **Structure:** Translucent canopy roof with open-air sides
- **Historic Role:** Host of the 2023 CFP National Championship Game (Georgia 65, TCU 7).

### NRG Stadium (Houston, TX)
- **Opened:** 2002 | **Capacity:** 72,220
- **Playing Surface:** Artificial Turf | **Structure:** Retractable fabric roof
- **Historic Role:** Host of the 2024 CFP National Championship Game (Michigan 34, Washington 13).

### Lucas Oil Stadium (Indianapolis, IN)
- **Opened:** 2008 | **Capacity:** 67,000
- **Playing Surface:** FieldTurf | **Structure:** Retractable roof and massive north window
- **Historic Role:** Annual host of the Big Ten Championship Game; host of the 2022 CFP National Championship Game (Georgia 33, Alabama 18).

## 3. Environmental & Venue Dynamics

### Surface Technology & Traction Profiles
- **Natural Turf Management:** Top collegiate grass stadiums (Sanford Stadium, Bryant-Denny, Rose Bowl) employ sand-based rootzones, sub-air vacuum cooling and drainage systems, and Bermuda grass overseeded with perennial ryegrass for optimal winter traction.
- **Modern Slit-Film & Monofilament Artificial Turfs:** Artificial surfaces (FieldTurf, Matrix Turf) feature cryogenic rubber and silica sand infills designed to reduce lower-extremity torque and maintain consistent ball bounce regardless of freezing Midwestern precipitation.

### Altitude Variations
- High-altitude venues like BYU's LaVell Edwards Stadium (4,649 ft), Utah's Rice-Eccles Stadium (4,637 ft), Colorado's Folsom Field (5,360 ft), and Wyoming's War Memorial Stadium (7,220 ft) feature thinner air density, increasing kicking distances by 5–8% while placing extreme metabolic demands on unacclimated visiting teams.

### Acoustic Architecture & Decibel Telemetry
- Cantilevered roofs (Husky Stadium), sunken bowl excavations (Autzen Stadium, The Swamp), and multi-tiered vertical grandstands (Tiger Stadium, Kyle Field, Beaver Stadium) regularly generate sustained in-game sound pressure levels between 110 and 130 decibels, severely impeding offensive pre-snap audibles and snap counts.

- **Sources:** NCAA Stadium Database; College Football Hall of Fame; Sports Turf Managers Association (STMA); National Weather Service.
