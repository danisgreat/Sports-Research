# IFAF World Championship — Historical Players, Rosters & Champions Register (1900–2025)

An authoritative chronicle of the IFAF World Championship of American Football, detailing participating nations, head coaches, tournament scores, MVPs, and medalists across all editions.

---

## Historical Timeline & Status (1900–2025)

- **Pre-Founding Era (1900 to 1998):**
  - **Status:** `NOT_YET_FOUNDED`
  - **Context:** The International Federation of American Football (IFAF) was formally established in 1998, with the inaugural world championship staged in Palermo, Italy in 1999. No international world championship sanctioned by IFAF existed prior to 1999.

- **Non-Tournament Years:**
  - The IFAF World Championship is a quadrennial tournament (held in 1999, 2003, 2007, 2011, and 2015). Intervening non-tournament years are classified as `NOT_SCHEDULED`.
  - The 2019 edition scheduled for Wollongong, Australia was postponed and subsequently cancelled (`NOT_HELD`).

## Chronological Register of Championship Tournaments

### 1999 IFAF World Championship
- **Host Location:** Velodromo Paolo Borsellino (Palermo, Italy)
- **Participating Nations (6 Teams):** Japan, Mexico, Sweden, Italy, Australia, Finland
- **Champions (Gold):** Japan (Head Coach: Toshiaki Abe (Head Coach) / Kiyoyuki Mori (OC))
- **Runners-up (Silver):** Mexico (Head Coach: Jacinto Licea (Head Coach))
- **Gold Medal Game:** Japan def. Mexico (6-0 (OT))
- **Tournament MVP:** Takashi Ogata (WR, Japan)
- **Senior Coaching Staffs:** Japan: Toshiaki Abe (Head Coach) / Kiyoyuki Mori (OC); Mexico: Jacinto Licea (Head Coach); Sweden: Jan Jenmert (Head Coach); Italy: Mike Wyatt (Head Coach); Australia: Jim Wagstaff (Head Coach); Finland: Tomas Hagman (Head Coach)

### 2003 IFAF World Championship
- **Host Location:** Herbert-Dröse-Stadion (Hanau, Germany)
- **Participating Nations (4 Teams):** Japan, Mexico, Germany, France
- **Champions (Gold):** Japan (Head Coach: Toshiaki Abe (Head Coach) / Kiyoyuki Mori (OC))
- **Runners-up (Silver):** Mexico (Head Coach: Diego García Miravete (Head Coach))
- **Gold Medal Game:** Japan def. Mexico (34-14)
- **Tournament MVP:** Tetsuo Takata (QB, Japan)
- **Senior Coaching Staffs:** Japan: Toshiaki Abe (Head Coach) / Kiyoyuki Mori (OC); Mexico: Diego García Miravete (Head Coach); Germany: Martin Hanselmann (Head Coach); France: Patrick Esume (Head Coach)

### 2007 IFAF World Championship
- **Host Location:** Todoroki Athletics Stadium (Kawasaki, Japan)
- **Participating Nations (6 Teams):** United States, Japan, Germany, Sweden, South Korea, France
- **Champions (Gold):** United States (Head Coach: John Mackovic (Head Coach))
- **Runners-up (Silver):** Japan (Head Coach: Toshiaki Abe (Head Coach) / Kiyoyuki Mori (OC))
- **Gold Medal Game:** United States def. Japan (23-20 (2OT))
- **Tournament MVP:** Craig Coffin (K/P, USA)
- **Senior Coaching Staffs:** United States: John Mackovic (Head Coach); Japan: Toshiaki Abe (Head Coach) / Kiyoyuki Mori (OC); Germany: Peter Springwald (Head Coach); Sweden: BD Kennedy (Head Coach); South Korea: Baek Seong-il (Sun-Il Baek) (Head Coach); France: Larry Legault (Head Coach)

### 2011 IFAF World Championship
- **Host Location:** Ernst-Happel-Stadion (Vienna, Austria)
- **Participating Nations (8 Teams):** United States, Canada, Japan, Mexico, Germany, France, Austria, Australia
- **Champions (Gold):** United States (Head Coach: Mel Tjeerdsma (Head Coach))
- **Runners-up (Silver):** Canada (Head Coach: Larry Haylor (Head Coach))
- **Gold Medal Game:** United States def. Canada (50-7)
- **Tournament MVP:** Nate Kmic (RB, USA)
- **Senior Coaching Staffs:** United States: Mel Tjeerdsma (Head Coach); Canada: Larry Haylor (Head Coach); Japan: Kiyoyuki Mori (Head Coach); Mexico: Raúl Rivera Sánchez (Head Coach); Germany: Peter Springwald (Head Coach); France: Larry Legault (Head Coach); Austria: Rick Rhoades (Head Coach); Australia: John Leijten (Head Coach)

### 2015 IFAF World Championship
- **Host Location:** Tom Benson Hall of Fame Stadium (Canton, Ohio, USA)
- **Participating Nations (7 Teams):** United States, Japan, Mexico, France, Australia, Brazil, South Korea
- **Champions (Gold):** United States (Head Coach: Dan Hawkins (Head Coach))
- **Runners-up (Silver):** Japan (Head Coach: Kiyoyuki Mori (Head Coach))
- **Gold Medal Game:** United States def. Japan (59-12)
- **Tournament MVP:** Kevin Burke (QB, USA)
- **Senior Coaching Staffs:** United States: Dan Hawkins (Head Coach); Japan: Kiyoyuki Mori (Head Coach); Mexico: Raúl Rivera Sánchez (Head Coach); France: Patrick Esume (Head Coach); Australia: John Leijten (Head Coach); Brazil: Danilo Müller (Head Coach); South Korea: Baek Seong-il (Sun-Il Baek) (Head Coach)
