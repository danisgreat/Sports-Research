# MLB Ballpark Profiles & Environmental Factors

Comprehensive guide to all 30 modern Major League ballparks and legendary historical ballparks from 1900 through 2025.

---

## 1. Modern MLB Ballparks

### Fenway Park — Boston, Massachusetts
- **Club:** Boston Red Sox | **Opened:** 1912 | **Capacity:** 37,755
- **Dimensions:** LF: 310 ft, The Green Monster: 37 ft high; CF: 390 ft; Deep CF (The Triangle): 420 ft; RF: 302 ft ("Pesky's Pole").
- **Observations:** Extreme doubles park; right-handed pull hitters turn fly-ball outs into off-the-wall singles and doubles. Short right-field line benefits left-handed pull hitters.

### Wrigley Field — Chicago, Illinois
- **Club:** Chicago Cubs | **Opened:** 1914 | **Capacity:** 41,649
- **Dimensions:** LF: 355 ft, CF: 400 ft, RF: 353 ft. Ivy-covered brick walls.
- **Observations:** Entirely dictated by Lake Michigan wind vectors.
  - "Wind blowing out to CF" (15-25 mph): Turns medium fly balls into home runs (run totals inflate to 12-16).
  - "Wind blowing in off the lake": Ball is knocked down; game suppresses into a low-scoring pitcher's duel.

### Coors Field — Denver, Colorado
- **Club:** Colorado Rockies | **Opened:** 1995 | **Capacity:** 50,144
- **Dimensions:** LF: 347 ft, CF: 415 ft, RF: 350 ft. Outfield area is massive (largest in MLB).
- **Altitude:** 5,197 ft (1,584 m) above sea level.
- **Observations:** Thin air allows batted balls to travel ~9% farther and reduces pitch movement (curveballs break 20% less; sliders stay flat). Massive outfield creates highest BABIP and batting averages in baseball. Humidor implemented in 2002 stabilized baseball moisture.

### Oracle Park — San Francisco, California
- **Club:** San Francisco Giants | **Opened:** 2000 | **Capacity:** 41,265
- **Dimensions:** LF: 339 ft, CF: 399 ft, RF: 309 ft (McCovey Cove).
- **Observations:** Dense marine air off San Francisco Bay and howling cross-winds from left-center to right field knock down right-handed fly balls; traditionally one of baseball's most severe pitcher's parks.

### Dodger Stadium — Los Angeles, California
- **Club:** Los Angeles Dodgers | **Opened:** 1962 | **Capacity:** 56,000
- **Dimensions:** LF: 330 ft, CF: 395 ft, RF: 330 ft.
- **Observations:** Sits in Chavez Ravine. Day games are warm and carry well; night games feature a cooling marine inversion layer that suppresses ball flight after the 5th inning.

### Yankee Stadium — Bronx, New York
- **Club:** New York Yankees | **Opened:** 2009 | **Capacity:** 46,537
- **Dimensions:** LF: 318 ft, CF: 408 ft, RF: 314 ft (The "Short Porch" in Right Field).
- **Observations:** Short right field (314 ft with an 8-ft wall) heavily incentivizes left-handed pull power; wind tunnel off the right-field upper deck creates upward draft.

## 2. Historic Major League Ballparks

- **Ebbets Field (Brooklyn Dodgers, 1913–1957):** Intimate dimensions, concave right-field wall.
- **Polo Grounds (New York Giants):** 258 ft down RF, 279 ft down LF, 483 ft to center dead-end.
- **Forbes Field (Pittsburgh, 1909–1970):** Sprawling park, 457 ft to deep left-center.
- **Tiger Stadium (Detroit, 1912–1999):** Upper deck overhung right field by 10 feet.
- **Sportsman's Park (St. Louis), Shibe Park/Connie Mack Stadium (Philadelphia), Crosley Field (Cincinnati), Comiskey Park (Chicago), Memorial Stadium (Baltimore), Astrodome (Houston - the 8th Wonder of the World), Exhibition Stadium (Toronto).**
