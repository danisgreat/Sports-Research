# EuroCup Basketball: Historical Players, Awards & Roster Register (1975–2026)

## 1. Overview & Award Structure
EuroCup Basketball represents the launchpad and proving ground for European and international basketball superstars. Throughout its history spanning the FIBA Saporta/Cup Winners' Cup era (1975–2002), the ULEB Cup era (2002–2008), and the modern EuroCup era (2008–2026), the competition has celebrated individual brilliance through prestigious annual awards:
- **EuroCup Regular Season MVP:** Awarded to the player with the greatest overall impact and performance index rating (PIR) across the regular season and Top 16.
- **EuroCup Finals MVP:** Awarded to the decisive performer in the championship final or final series.
- **EuroCup Rising Star Trophy:** Awarded to the premier player under the age of 22 at the start of the season.
- **EuroCup Coach of the Year:** Awarded to the tactician orchestrating the tournament's most exceptional team achievement.

## 6. Legendary Performances of the Historical Era (1975–2002)

### Dražen Petrović's 62-Point Masterpiece (March 14, 1989 — Piraeus, Greece)
In the 1988–89 FIBA European Cup Winners' Cup final between Real Madrid and Snaidero Caserta at the Peace and Friendship Stadium in Piraeus, Croatian maestro Dražen Petrović produced arguably the most legendary scoring exhibition in European club basketball history. Petrović poured in **62 points** (shooting 12/14 2FG, 8/16 3FG, and 14/15 FT). Brazilian scoring legend Oscar Schmidt countered with **44 points** for Caserta, pushing the contest into overtime before Real Madrid prevailed 117–113.

### Pierluigi Marzorati's Cantù Hegemony (1976–1981)
Italian floor general Pierluigi Marzorati orchestrated three consecutive European Cup Winners' Cup triumphs for Forst/Gabetti Cantù (1977, 1978, 1979) and a fourth in 1981, defining fast-break transition basketball and pick-and-pop perimeter spacing across Europe.
