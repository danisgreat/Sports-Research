# EuroCup Basketball: Historical Teams, Franchises & Rosters (1975–2026)

## 1. Overview & Lineage of European Secondary Club Basketball
European men's secondary top-tier club basketball has evolved across six distinct organizational eras spanning 52 seasons from 1975 to 2026:

1. **FIBA European Cup Winners' Cup (1975–1991):** Organized by FIBA Europe as the second-tier continental tournament behind the FIBA European Champions Cup. Contested by national domestic cup winners across Western, Eastern, and Southern Europe in two-legged knockout ties leading to a quarterfinal group stage, two-legged semifinals, and a neutral single-game final.
2. **FIBA European Cup (1991–1996):** Expanded FIBA tournament broadening participation to domestic league runners-up and top national cup performers following the collapse of the Eastern Bloc and dissolution of Yugoslavia and the Soviet Union.
3. **FIBA EuroCup (1996–1998):** Rebranded secondary competition incorporating preliminary round-robin groups, 32-team knockout stages, and prominent continental powers.
4. **FIBA Saporta Cup (1998–2002):** Named in honor of legendary FIBA official Raimundo Saporta. Served as the definitive second-tier European cup before the historic consolidation of European club basketball.
5. **ULEB Cup (2002–2008):** Established by the Union of European Leagues of Basketball (ULEB) and Euroleague Basketball following the discontinuation of the Saporta Cup and Korać Cup. Transitioned the second-tier continental landscape to modern professional league governance, commercial broadcast distribution, and direct promotion to the EuroLeague.
6. **Eurocup Basketball / 7DAYS EuroCup / BKT EuroCup (2008–2026):** Rebranded under Euroleague Basketball governance. Modern format features 20 to 24 premier clubs divided into two regional round-robin groups playing 18 rounds, followed by high-stakes single-elimination play-in/playoffs and best-of-three finals, with the champions (and often finalists) earning promotion to the Turkish Airlines EuroLeague.

## 3. Prominent Clubs & Historical Profiles

### Valencia Basket Club (Spain) — The All-Time Titans
- **EuroCup Titles:** 4 (2002–03, 2009–10, 2013–14, 2018–19) — *All-time record*
- **Finalists:** 2 (2011–12, 2016–17)
- **Key Figures:** Dejan Tomašević, Fabricio Oberto, Víctor Claver, Matt Nielsen, Bojan Dubljević, Justin Doellman, Sam Van Rossom, Fernando San Emeterio, Jaume Ponsarnau, Pedro Martínez.
- **Legacy:** Valencia built its modern European identity through the ULEB/EuroCup. Winning four continental crowns earned multiple promotions into the EuroLeague and established "La Fonteta" as one of the most formidable basketball cauldrons in Europe.

### BC Lietuvos Rytas / BC Rytas Vilnius (Lithuania)
- **EuroCup Titles:** 2 (2004–05, 2008–09)
- **Finalists:** 1 (2006–07)
- **Key Figures:** Robertas Javtokas, Marijonas Petravičius, Chuck Eidson, Jonas Valančiūnas, Tomas Delininkaitis, Rimas Kurtinaitis, Tomo Mahorič.
- **Legacy:** The powerhouse from Vilnius won two ULEB/EuroCup titles with thrilling high-tempo play and exceptional Lithuanian frontcourts, culminating in Chuck Eidson's 2009 MVP campaign.

### BC Khimki (Russia)
- **EuroCup Titles:** 2 (2011–12, 2014–15)
- **Finalists:** 1 (2008–09)
- **Key Figures:** Zoran Planinić, Vitaly Fridzon, Sergey Monia, Paul Davis, Tyrese Rice, Petteri Koponen, Rimas Kurtinaitis.
- **Legacy:** Under Lithuanian coach Rimas Kurtinaitis, Khimki dominated the 2012 and 2015 tournaments with high-powered backcourts and precision perimeter execution.

### Real Madrid Baloncesto (Spain)
- **Saporta / European Cup Titles:** 4 (1983–84, 1988–89, 1991–92, 1996–97)
- **ULEB Cup Titles:** 1 (2006–07)
- **Key Figures:** Wayne Brabender, Dražen Petrović, Arvydas Sabonis, Alberto Herreros, Louis Bullock, Felipe Reyes, Charles Smith, Joan Plaza.
- **Legacy:** Real Madrid's 1989 Cup Winners' Cup final in Piraeus is universally regarded as one of the greatest games in European basketball history, where Dražen Petrović exploded for 62 points in a 117–113 overtime victory over Oscar Schmidt's Snaidero Caserta. In 2007, Real Madrid captured the ULEB Cup behind Charles Smith and Louis Bullock.

### Pallacanestro Cantù (Italy)
- **Cup Winners' Cup Titles:** 4 (1976–77, 1977–78, 1978–79, 1980–81) — *Historic record era*
- **Key Figures:** Pierluigi Marzorati, Carlo Recalcati, Fabrizio Della Fiori, Renzo Bariviera, Antonello Riva, Arnaldo Taurisano.
- **Legacy:** Cantù produced an unmatched dynasty in the late 1970s, winning three consecutive Cup Winners' Cups from 1977 to 1979 and a fourth in 1981, anchored by the legendary Italian point guard Pierluigi Marzorati.

### Paris Basketball (France)
- **EuroCup Titles:** 1 (2023–24)
- **Key Figures:** TJ Shorts II, Nadir Hifi, Collin Malcolm, Tyson Ward, Mikael Jantunen, Tuomas Iisalo.
- **Legacy:** Paris Basketball orchestrated the most statistically dominant campaign in EuroCup history in 2023–24 under Finnish head coach Tuomas Iisalo, posting an astonishing 22–1 record and sweeping London Lions and JL Bourg to claim the championship and earn EuroLeague promotion.

## 4. Competitive Structure & Format Evolution

### Round-Robin Groups to Single-Elimination
Historically, EuroCup utilized a two-phase group stage (Regular Season and Top 16 / Last 32), followed by two-legged home-and-away knockout ties decided on aggregate points. In 2021, Euroleague Basketball revolutionized the tournament format:
- Two 10-team regional groups (Groups A and B) playing 18 regular-season rounds.
- Top 6 teams from each group advance (top 2 receive first-round byes).
- Single-elimination Eighth-finals and Quarterfinals hosted at the home court of the higher-seeded club, delivering immense win-or-go-home drama.
- Best-of-three Semifinals and Finals determining the champion and EuroLeague promotion berths.
