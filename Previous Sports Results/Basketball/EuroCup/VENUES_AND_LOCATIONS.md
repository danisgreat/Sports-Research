# EuroCup Basketball: Venues & Geographic Locations (1975–2026)

## 1. Geographic Scope & Continental Reach
EuroCup Basketball and its lineage predecessors represent one of the geographically broadest club competitions in global sport. The tournament links basketball capitals across Western Europe, the Iberian Peninsula, the Mediterranean, Central Europe, the Baltic States, the Balkan Peninsula, Eastern Europe, Turkey, and the Middle East.

Over the 52 seasons from 1975 to 2026, clubs from more than 28 distinct European nations and territories have contested the championship.

---

## 2. Regional Breakdown & Country Distribution

### Iberian Region (Spain & Portugal)
- **Hub Cities:** Valencia, Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, Badalona, Málaga, Bilbao, Zaragoza, Madrid, Vitoria-Gasteiz, Seville, Fuenlabrada, Andorra la Vella.
- **Key Venues:** Pabellón Fuente de San Luis, Gran Canaria Arena, Palau Olímpic de Badalona, Martín Carpena, Bilbao Arena, Poliesportiu d'Andorra.
- **Regional Characteristics:** Known for rapid transition basketball, heavy pick-and-roll execution, deep fan cultures, and dominant tournament performance (winning 8 ULEB/EuroCup titles since 2002).

### Italian Peninsula
- **Hub Cities:** Bologna, Milan, Cantù, Pesaro, Varese, Caserta, Treviso, Siena, Venice/Mestre, Trento, Rome, Brescia, Reggio Emilia.
- **Key Venues:** Virtus Segafredo Arena, PalaDozza, PalaTaliercio, PalaTrento, PalaLeonessa, PalaWhirlpool.
- **Regional Characteristics:** Tactical precision, tactical half-court execution, and rich historical heritage dating back to the late-1970s Cantù European Cup Winners' Cup dynasty.

### French & Benelux Region
- **Hub Cities:** Paris, Bourg-en-Bresse, Villeurbanne/Lyon, Strasbourg, Limoges, Monaco, Charleroi, Ostend, Antwerp.
- **Key Venues:** Adidas Arena, Halle Georges Carpentier, Ekinox, Astroballe, Rhénus Sport, Spiroudome, Versluys Dôme.
- **Regional Characteristics:** High-athleticism, dynamic transition pace, modern arena development (culminating in Paris Basketball's 2024 EuroCup triumph and EuroLeague ascent).

### Adriatic & Western Balkans (Former Yugoslavia)
- **Hub Cities:** Belgrade (Serbia), Podgorica (Montenegro), Ljubljana (Slovenia), Zagreb (Croatia), Zadar (Croatia), Bar (Montenegro), Novo Mesto (Slovenia), Sarajevo (Bosnia and Herzegovina).
- **Key Venues:** Aleksandar Nikolić Hall (Pionir), Štark Arena, Morača Sports Center, Arena Stožice, Tivoli Hall, Dražen Petrović Basketball Hall, Leon Štukelj Hall.
- **Regional Characteristics:** Renowned for world-class technical fundamentals, passionate vocal fan bases, and high tactical discipline.

### Baltic & Northern European Region
- **Hub Cities:** Vilnius (Lithuania), Panevėžys (Lithuania), Klaipėda (Lithuania), Kaunas (Lithuania), Riga (Latvia), Tallinn (Estonia), London (United Kingdom).
- **Key Venues:** Avia Solutions Group Arena, Kalnapilio Arena, Švyturys Arena, Arena Riga, Copper Box Arena.
- **Regional Characteristics:** Elite perimeter shooting, physical interior defense, and fervent national basketball devotion in Lithuania.

### Anatolian & Turkish Region
- **Hub Cities:** Istanbul, Ankara, Bursa, İzmir.
- **Key Venues:** Sinan Erdem Dome, Volkswagen Arena, Ankara Arena, Tofaş Nilüfer Spor Salonu, BJK Akatlar Arena.
- **Regional Characteristics:** Massive basketball followings, fervent crowd atmospheres, and substantial investment that yielded titles for Galatasaray (2016) and Darüşşafaka (2018).

### Central & Eastern European Region
- **Hub Cities:** Neu-Ulm (Germany), Berlin (Germany), Hamburg (Germany), Munich (Germany), Wrocław (Poland), Zielona Góra (Poland), Włocławek (Poland), Moscow (Russia), Kazan (Russia), Krasnodar (Russia), Saint Petersburg (Russia), Jerusalem (Israel), Tel Aviv (Israel).
- **Key Venues:** ratiopharm arena, Mercedes-Benz Arena, Hala Stulecia, Basket-Hall Kazan, Basket-Hall Krasnodar, Pais Arena Jerusalem.
- **Regional Characteristics:** Multi-faceted blend of physical interior rebounding, international lineups, and modern entertainment arenas.

## 3. Neutral-Court Finals & Host City Heritage

The secondary European club competition historically rotated neutral final venues across Europe to showcase the sport:
- **1976:** Turin (Italy)
- **1977 & 1983:** Palma de Mallorca (Spain)
- **1978 & 1980:** Milan (Italy)
- **1979:** Poreč (Yugoslavia / Croatia)
- **1981:** Rome (Italy)
- **1982:** Brussels (Belgium)
- **1984:** Ostend (Belgium)
- **1985 & 1988:** Grenoble (France)
- **1986:** Caserta (Italy)
- **1987:** Novi Sad (Yugoslavia / Serbia)
- **1989:** Piraeus (Greece)
- **1990:** Florence (Italy)
- **1991:** Geneva (Switzerland)
- **1992:** Nantes (France)
- **1993:** Turin (Italy)
- **1994 & 2000:** Lausanne (Switzerland)
- **1995:** Istanbul (Turkey)
- **1996:** Vitoria-Gasteiz (Spain)
- **1997:** Nicosia (Cyprus)
- **1998:** Belgrade (FR Yugoslavia)
- **1999:** Zaragoza (Spain)
- **2001:** Warsaw (Poland)
- **2002:** Lyon (France)
- **2004–2007, 2013:** Charleroi (Belgium)
- **2008 & 2009:** Turin (Italy)
- **2010:** Vitoria-Gasteiz (Spain)
- **2011:** Treviso (Italy)
- **2012:** Khimki (Russia)
- **2022:** Bologna (Italy)
- **2023:** Las Palmas de Gran Canaria (Spain)
