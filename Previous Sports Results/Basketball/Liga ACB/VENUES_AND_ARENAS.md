# Spanish Top-Flight Basketball: Venues & Arenas Directory (1975–2026)

## 1. Overview
Spanish professional basketball is staged in some of Europe's largest, most technologically sophisticated indoor sports pavilions. The Asociación de Clubes de Baloncesto (ACB) mandates strict minimum seating capacities (minimum 5,000 seats for modern participation), certified hardwood flooring, instant replay monitors, LED scoreboards, and broadcast facilities.

---

## 2. Major Arena Profiles

### WiZink Center (Palacio de Deportes de la Comunidad de Madrid)
- **Location:** Plaza Salvador Dalí / Avenida de Felipe II, Madrid
- **Capacity:** 15,000 (Basketball configuration) / 17,453 (Concerts)
- **Primary Tenants:** Real Madrid Baloncesto (2011–present), CB Estudiantes (2006–present)
- **Historical Significance:** Rebuilt following a catastrophic fire in 2001; reopened in 2005. Hosted the 2007 EuroBasket Final, 2014 FIBA Basketball World Cup Final, 2008 and 2015 EuroLeague Final Fours, and 12 Copas del Rey.

### Fernando Buesa Arena
- **Location:** Carretera de Zurbano, Vitoria-Gasteiz, Basque Country
- **Capacity:** 15,716
- **Primary Tenant:** Saski Baskonia (1991–present)
- **Historical Significance:** Originally opened as *Pabellón Araba* with 5,400 seats; expanded in 1999 to 9,400 seats and named after assassinated politician Fernando Buesa. Underwent a major vertical expansion in 2011–12, hoisting a 16-sided jumbotron and expanding to 15,716 seats in just 5 months. Hosted the 2019 EuroLeague Final Four and five Copas del Rey (2000, 2002, 2008, 2013, 2017).

### Palau Municipal d'Esports de Badalona (Palau Olímpic)
- **Location:** Carrer de Ponent, Badalona, Catalonia
- **Capacity:** 12,760
- **Primary Tenant:** Club Joventut Badalona (1991–present)
- **Historical Significance:** Award-winning architecture designed by Esteve Bonell and Francesc Rius for the 1992 Barcelona Olympic Games. Venue of the legendary 1992 USA "Dream Team" Olympic basketball tournament (Jordan, Magic, Bird). Hosted the 2023 Copa del Rey.

### Gran Canaria Arena
- **Location:** Calle Fondos de Segura, Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, Canary Islands
- **Capacity:** 11,500
- **Primary Tenant:** CB Gran Canaria (2014–present)
- **Historical Significance:** Purpose-built for the 2014 FIBA Basketball World Cup; features modern VIP lounges, LED rings, and high-efficiency Atlantic maritime climate control. Hosted the 2015 and 2018 Copas del Rey and the 2023 EuroCup Finals.

### Palacio de Deportes José María Martín Carpena
- **Location:** Calle Miguel de Mérida Nicolich, Málaga, Andalusia
- **Capacity:** 11,300
- **Primary Tenant:** Baloncesto Málaga / Unicaja (1999–present)
- **Historical Significance:** Originally opened with 9,743 seats as *Palacio de Deportes de Málaga*; expanded in 2010 to 11,300. Renamed in honor of councilor José María Martín Carpena. Hosted four Copas del Rey (2001, 2007, 2014, 2020) and the 2024 Supercopa Endesa.

### Donostia Arena 2016 (Plaza de Toros de Illunbe)
- **Location:** Paseo de Miramón, San Sebastián, Basque Country
- **Capacity:** 11,000
- **Primary Tenant:** Gipuzkoa Basket (2006–2020)
- **Historical Significance:** Dual-purpose converted bullring featuring a motorized retractable roof and amphitheater acoustics.

### Pabellón Príncipe Felipe
- **Location:** Avenida Cesáreo Alierta, Zaragoza, Aragon
- **Capacity:** 10,744
- **Primary Tenant:** Basket Zaragoza 2002 (Casademont Zaragoza)
- **Historical Significance:** Opened in 1990; hosted the 1990 and 1995 EuroLeague Final Fours and the 1991, 2005, and 2008 Copas del Rey.

### Bilbao Arena (Miribilla)
- **Location:** Askatasuna Hiribidea, Bilbao, Basque Country
- **Capacity:** 10,014
- **Primary Tenant:** Bilbao Basket (2010–present)
- **Historical Significance:** Designed by ACXT architects atop the former Miribilla iron mines; known as "El Efecto Miribilla" for its steep rake and ferocious acoustics. Hosted the 2022 Basketball Champions League Final Four.

### Pabellón Municipal Fuente de San Luis ("La Fonteta") & Roig Arena
- **Location:** Avinguda dels Germans Maristes, Valencia
- **Capacity:** 9,000 (La Fonteta) / 15,600 (Roig Arena, opening 2025)
- **Primary Tenant:** Valencia Basket Club
- **Historical Significance:** La Fonteta was the epicenter of Valencia's 2017 Liga ACB championship and four EuroCup titles (2003, 2010, 2014, 2019). Hosted the 2020 COVID-19 biosecure ACB "Fase Final Extraordinaria" bubble tournament. Replaced by the privately funded state-of-the-art Roig Arena.

### Palau Blaugrana
- **Location:** Avinguda d'Arístides Maillol, Barcelona, Catalonia
- **Capacity:** 7,585
- **Primary Tenant:** FC Barcelona Bàsquet (1971–present)
- **Historical Significance:** Renowned for its low dome roof, intimate proximity to the court, and intimidating home-court advantage. Witnessed 19 domestic Liga titles, dozens of European nights, and the retired jerseys of Epi, Solozábal, Jiménez, Dueñas, and Juan Carlos Navarro.

### Coliseum da Coruña
- **Location:** Rúa Francisco Pérez Carballo, A Coruña, Galicia
- **Capacity:** 9,300 (Basketball configuration) / 11,000
- **Primary Tenant:** Leyma Básquet Coruña (2024–present)
- **Historical Significance:** Multipurpose arena that hosted the 1993 and 2016 Copas del Rey, and became a top-flight home venue with Coruña's historic promotion in 2024.
