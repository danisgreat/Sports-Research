## 2. Autonomous Community Breakdown

### 1. Catalonia (Catalunya)
- **Primary Urban Centers:** Barcelona, Badalona, Manresa, Girona, Lleida, L'Hospitalet de Llobregat, Granollers, Santa Coloma de Gramenet, Pineda de Mar, Mollet del Vallès, Mataró
- **Franchises:** FC Barcelona Bàsquet, Club Joventut Badalona, BAXI Manresa, Bàsquet Girona, Hiopos Lleida, CB L'Hospitalet, Círcol Catòlic Badalona (Cotonificio), CB Granollers, CB Santa Coloma, UDR Pineda, CB Mollet, RCD Espanyol
- **Historical Significance:** The historical cradle of Spanish basketball. Spanish basketball was first introduced in Catalonia in 1921 by Father Eusebio Millán at the Escuelas Pías of Sant Antoni in Barcelona.

### 2. Community of Madrid (Comunidad de Madrid)
- **Primary Urban Centers:** Madrid, Fuenlabrada, Collado Villalba
- **Franchises:** Real Madrid Baloncesto, Club Baloncesto Estudiantes, Baloncesto Fuenlabrada, CB Collado Villalba (Atlético Madrid Villalba), CB Inmobanco (Tempus), Real Canoe NC
- **Historical Significance:** Home to the two most decorated multi-decade dynasties (Real Madrid) and beloved student academy institutions (Estudiantes), with southern and sierra hubs in Fuenlabrada and Villalba.

### 3. Basque Country (Euskadi)
- **Primary Urban Centers:** Vitoria-Gasteiz (Álava), Bilbao (Biscay), San Sebastián (Gipuzkoa)
- **Franchises:** Saski Baskonia (Fernando Buesa Arena), Surne Bilbao Basket (Bilbao Arena), Gipuzkoa Basket (Donostia Arena), Askatuak SBT (Gasca)
- **Historical Significance:** Renowned for fierce local rivalries ("Derbi Vasco"), extraordinary home fan loyalty, and European consistency.

### 4. Andalusia (Andalucía)
- **Primary Urban Centers:** Málaga, Seville, Granada, Huelva
- **Franchises:** Baloncesto Málaga (Unicaja), Real Betis Baloncesto (CB Sevilla / Caja San Fernando), Fundación CB Granada (Covirán Granada), CB Ciudad de Huelva
- **Historical Significance:** Southern powerhouse anchored by Unicaja Málaga, hosting major European campaigns and memorable playoff battles.

### 5. Valencian Community (Comunitat Valenciana)
- **Primary Urban Centers:** Valencia, Alicante, Llíria
- **Franchises:** Valencia Basket Club, Lucentum Alicante, CB Llíria (Choleck)
- **Historical Significance:** Rapid rise since the 1990s through Valencia Basket (Juan Roig investment) into an elite domestic champion and EuroLeague staple.

### 6. Canary Islands (Islas Canarias)
- **Primary Urban Centers:** Las Palmas de Gran Canaria (Gran Canaria), San Cristóbal de La Laguna / Santa Cruz de Tenerife (Tenerife)
- **Franchises:** CB Gran Canaria (Dreamland Gran Canaria), CB 1939 Canarias (Lenovo Tenerife), RC Náutico de Tenerife, Tenerife CB (Unelco)
- **Historical Significance:** Atlantic island basketball hotbed. The *Derbi Canario* between Gran Canaria and Tenerife is one of European basketball's most passionate fixtures. Travel involves mandatory 2.5 to 3-hour flights from mainland Spain.

### 7. Galicia
- **Primary Urban Centers:** Lugo, Santiago de Compostela, A Coruña, Ourense, Ferrol
- **Franchises:** CB Breogán (Río Breogán), Obradoiro CAB (Monbus Obradoiro), Básquet Coruña (Leyma Coruña), Club Ourense Baloncesto (COB), OAR Ferrol (Clesa Ferrol)
- **Historical Significance:** Proud northwestern basketball tradition characterized by passionate sellouts at Pazo dos Deportes and Fontes do Sar.

### 8. Castile and León (Castilla y León)
- **Primary Urban Centers:** Valladolid, Palencia, León, Salamanca
- **Franchises:** CB Valladolid (Fórum Filatélico), Palencia Baloncesto (Zunder Palencia), CB León, CB Salamanca
- **Historical Significance:** Traditional meseta clubs; Valladolid hosted Arvydas Sabonis from 1989 to 1992; Palencia reached the top flight in 2023.

### 9. Aragon (Aragón)
- **Primary Urban Centers:** Zaragoza, Huesca
- **Franchises:** Basket Zaragoza 2002 / CB Zaragoza (CAI / Casademont Zaragoza), CB Peñas Huesca (Magia de Huesca)
- **Historical Significance:** Pabellón Príncipe Felipe hosted historic Copa del Rey and EuroLeague Final Four tournaments.

### 10. Region of Murcia (Región de Murcia)
- **Primary Urban Center:** Murcia
- **Franchises:** Club Baloncesto Murcia (UCAM Murcia)
- **Historical Significance:** Southeastern stronghold that reached the 2024 Liga ACB Finals.

### 11. Balearic Islands (Illes Balears)
- **Primary Urban Center:** Mahón (Menorca)
- **Franchises:** Menorca Bàsquet (ViveMenorca)
- **Historical Significance:** Competed in Liga ACB from 2005 to 2011 at Pavelló Menorca.

### 12. Cantabria & Asturias
- **Primary Urban Centers:** Santander (Cantabria), Gijón (Asturias)
- **Franchises:** Cantabria Baloncesto (Lobos Cantabria), Gijón Baloncesto (Cabitel Gijón)
- **Historical Significance:** Northern coastal representatives in top-flight basketball during the 1990s and early 2000s.

### 13. Extremadura
- **Primary Urban Center:** Cáceres
- **Franchises:** Cáceres CB (Pabellón Multiusos)
- **Historical Significance:** 1997 Copa del Rey finalists and Korać Cup participants.

### 14. Principality of Andorra (Country in Pyrenees)
- **Primary Urban Center:** Andorra la Vella
- **Franchises:** Bàsquet Club Andorra (MoraBanc Andorra)
- **Historical Significance:** Sovereign Microstate competing in Spanish domestic basketball system by special federation affiliation agreement. Located at 1,023 meters elevation in the Pyrenees.

## 3. Travel Logistics & High-Elevation Venues
1. **Canary Islands Travel Protocol:** Visiting mainland teams fly into Gran Canaria Airport (LPA) or Tenerife North Airport (TFN) with a minimum 2.5-hour scheduled flight time and 1-hour time zone shift (WET / UTC+0).
2. **Pyrenean Elevation (Andorra):** Poliesportiu d'Andorra sits at 1,023 meters above sea level, requiring mountain travel from Toulouse or Barcelona airports.
3. **High-Speed Rail Connectivity (AVE):** Most mainland ties (Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia, Zaragoza, Seville, Málaga, Alicante, Valladolid, Córdoba) are connected via high-speed train networks, minimizing domestic flight fatigue.
