# Previous Sports Results (1900–2025)

An authoritative, comprehensive research directory and historical archive of competitive sports results, covering the modern era from **1900 through 2025** across 17 distinct sporting disciplines and two competition tiers.

---

## Directory Architecture

The archive is organized with **sport categories** at the root, containing dedicated subfolders for each **specific league, tournament, or championship**, which in turn contain individual **year subfolders from 1900 through 2025** (126 years):

```
Previous Sports Results/
  └── <Sport>/                        # 17 sport disciplines
        └── <Competition or League>/  # Dedicated tournament / competition folder
              └── <Year>/             # 1900 through 2025 (126 years)
                    └── .gitkeep      # Git-tracked archive placeholder
```

## 1. Soccer (Association Football)

### International Competitions (`international/`)

#### Men's FIFA World Cup
- **Governing Body:** Fédération Internationale de Football Association (FIFA)
- **Inaugural Edition:** 1930 (Uruguay)
- **Cycle & Format:** Quadrennial. Group stages followed by single-elimination knockout rounds (expanded from 16 to 24 in 1982, 32 in 1998, and 48 in 2026).
- **Trophy:** FIFA World Cup Trophy (Jules Rimet Trophy prior to 1974).
- **Most Successful Nations:** Brazil (5 titles), Germany (4), Italy (4), Argentina (3), France (2), Uruguay (2).
- **Summary:** The pinnacle of association football and the most watched sporting event globally. National teams qualify through six continental confederations (UEFA, CONMEBOL, CONCACAF, CAF, AFC, OFC).
- **Historical Hosts (Recent):**
  - 1990: Italy
  - 1994: United States
  - 1998: France
  - 2002: South Korea & Japan
  - 2006: Germany
  - 2010: South Africa
  - 2014: Brazil
  - 2018: Russia
  - 2022: Qatar
  - 2026: Canada, Mexico & United States

#### Women's FIFA World Cup
- **Governing Body:** FIFA
- **Inaugural Edition:** 1991 (China)
- **Cycle & Format:** Quadrennial (held the year following the men's event since 1991; 32 teams since 2023).
- **Trophy:** FIFA Women's World Cup Trophy.
- **Most Successful Nations:** United States (4 titles), Germany (2), Japan (1), Norway (1), Spain (1).
- **Summary:** The premier international championship in women's football. It has experienced rapid exponential growth in viewership, professionalization, and tactical evolution.
- **Historical Hosts (Recent):**
  - 1991: China
  - 1995: Sweden
  - 1999: United States
  - 2003: United States
  - 2007: China
  - 2011: Germany
  - 2015: Canada
  - 2019: France
  - 2023: Australia & New Zealand
  - 2027: Brazil *(Upcoming)*

#### UEFA European Championship (Men's & Women's Euro)
- **Governing Body:** Union of European Football Associations (UEFA)
- **Inaugural Edition:** Men's: 1960 (France); Women's: 1984.
- **Cycle & Format:** Quadrennial. 24 teams (Men's since 2016); 16 teams (Women's).
- **Trophy:** Henri Delaunay Trophy (Men's).
- **Most Successful Nations:** Men's: Spain (4 titles), Germany (3); Women's: Germany (8 titles).
- **Summary:** Widely considered the most fiercely contested continental football tournament in the world, featuring Europe's elite national sides.
- **Men's Euro Hosts (Recent):**
  - 1988: West Germany
  - 1992: Sweden
  - 1996: England
  - 2000: Belgium & Netherlands
  - 2004: Portugal
  - 2008: Austria & Switzerland
  - 2012: Poland & Ukraine
  - 2016: France
  - 2021: Pan-European (11 nations)
  - 2024: Germany

#### Copa América
- **Governing Body:** Confederación Sudamericana de Fútbol (CONMEBOL)
- **Inaugural Edition:** 1916 (Argentina) — The oldest running continental football championship.
- **Cycle & Format:** Quadrennial / variable; includes 10 CONMEBOL nations plus invited confederation guests (frequently CONCACAF).
- **Most Successful Nations:** Argentina (16 titles), Uruguay (15), Brazil (9).
- **Summary:** The championship of South American national teams, famed for high intensity, technical prowess, and historic rivalries (e.g. Argentina vs. Brazil).
- **Historical Hosts (Recent):** 1999: Paraguay; 2001: Colombia; 2004: Peru; 2007: Venezuela; 2011: Argentina; 2015: Chile; 2016: United States (Centenario); 2019: Brazil; 2021: Brazil; 2024: United States.

#### Other Continental Championships & Olympics
- **Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON - CAF):** Established 1957. Africa's premier national tournament. Record winner: Egypt (7 titles). Recent hosts: 2019: Egypt, 2022: Cameroon, 2024: Ivory Coast, 2025–26: Morocco.
- **AFC Asian Cup (AFC):** Established 1956. Asia's continental championship. Record winner: Japan (4 titles). Recent hosts: 2011: Qatar, 2015: Australia, 2019: UAE, 2023: Qatar.
- **CONCACAF Gold Cup:** Established 1963 (revamped 1991). Governs North, Central American, and Caribbean national teams. Dominated by Mexico and the United States.
- **Olympic Football Tournament (IOC / FIFA):** Played at the Summer Olympics. Historically full senior squads (1900–1988) and under-23 with 3 overage players since 1992 for men; full senior national teams for women since 1996.

### National & Club Competitions (`national/`)

#### FIFA Club World Cup
- **Governing Body:** FIFA
- **Inaugural Edition:** 2000 (annual 7-team knockout until 2023; expanded quadrennial 32-team tournament starting 2025 in the United States).
- **Summary:** Brings together the champion clubs of each continental confederation (UEFA Champions League, Copa Libertadores, etc.) to crown the world club champion.

#### UEFA Champions League, Europa League & Conference League
- **Governing Body:** UEFA
- **Inaugural Edition:** European Cup founded 1955; rebranded as UEFA Champions League in 1992.
- **Format:** Top domestic league finishers compete through a league/group phase followed by two-legged knockouts and a neutral-venue single final.
- **Trophy:** European Champion Clubs' Cup ("Ol' Big Ears").
- **Dominant Clubs:** Real Madrid (15 titles), AC Milan (7), Bayern Munich (6), Liverpool (6), Barcelona (5).
- **Significance:** The undisputed highest level of tactical and technical football on Earth.

#### CONMEBOL Copa Libertadores
- **Governing Body:** CONMEBOL
- **Inaugural Edition:** 1960.
- **Summary:** South America's most prestigious club competition, matching top clubs across Argentina, Brazil, Colombia, Chile, Uruguay, and across the continent. Independiente (7 titles) and Boca Juniors (6 titles) lead historical tallies.

#### Premier Domestic Leagues
- **English Premier League (EPL):** Founded 1992 (breakaway from Football League First Division, est. 1888). Renowned for financial scale, global audience, and competitive parity across 20 clubs.
- **La Liga (Spain):** Founded 1929. Famous for the *El Clásico* rivalry between Real Madrid and FC Barcelona.
- **Serie A (Italy):** Founded 1898. Famed for defensive tactical rigor and storied clubs including Juventus, Inter Milan, and AC Milan.
- **Bundesliga (Germany):** Founded 1963. Recognized for 50+1 fan ownership model, high stadium attendances, and high-scoring matches.
- **Ligue 1 (France):** Founded 1932. Top French tier, historically dominated by Saint-Étienne, Marseille, and Paris Saint-Germain.
- **Major League Soccer (MLS):** Founded 1993 (first season 1996) in the USA and Canada. Closed single-entity league with regular season and MLS Cup Playoffs.
- **A-League Men & Women (Australia & New Zealand):** Established 2004. Primary professional league in Australasia.
- **Top Women's Leagues:** NWSL (United States), Women's Super League (WSL, England), Liga F (Spain), Frauen-Bundesliga (Germany), Première Ligue (France).

## 2. Basketball

### International Competitions (`international/`)

#### FIBA Basketball World Cup (Men's & Women's)
- **Governing Body:** Fédération Internationale de Basketball (FIBA)
- **Inaugural Edition:** Men's: 1950 (Argentina); Women's: 1953 (Chile).
- **Cycle & Format:** Quadrennial (adjusted in 2019 to non-Olympic years). 32 teams compete in multi-phase group stages leading to quarter-finals, semi-finals, and final.
- **Trophy:** Naismith Trophy.
- **Dominant Teams:** United States (5 titles), Yugoslavia/Serbia (5), Soviet Union (3), Spain (2).
- **Historical Hosts (Men's Recent):** 1986: Spain; 1990: Argentina; 1994: Canada; 1998: Greece; 2002: United States; 2006: Japan; 2010: Turkey; 2014: Spain; 2019: China; 2023: Indonesia, Japan & Philippines; 2027: Qatar *(Upcoming)*.

#### Olympic Basketball Tournament
- **Governing Body:** IOC / FIBA
- **Debut:** Men's: 1936 (Berlin); Women's: 1976 (Montreal).
- **Summary:** The gold standard of national basketball excellence. The 1992 Barcelona Games marked the entry of NBA professional players (the USA "Dream Team"), transforming international basketball. USA Men have captured 17 Olympic golds; USA Women have captured 10 consecutive/dominant Olympic golds.

#### Continental Championships
- **EuroBasket:** Europe's premier national competition, organized since 1935.
- **FIBA AmeriCup & FIBA Asia Cup:** Regional qualifying and continental title championships.

---

### National & Club Competitions (`national/`)

#### National Basketball Association (NBA)
- **Governing Body:** NBA Board of Governors
- **Inaugural Season:** 1946 (as Basketball Association of America, BAA; merged with NBL in 1949 to form NBA).
- **Geography:** 30 franchises (29 in the United States, 1 in Canada — Toronto Raptors).
- **Format:** 82-game regular season, NBA Play-In Tournament, and best-of-seven Eastern and Western Conference Playoffs culminating in the NBA Finals.
- **Trophy:** Larry O'Brien Championship Trophy.
- **Significance:** The world's preeminent professional basketball league in athletic capability, financial power, and global reach. Boston Celtics and Los Angeles Lakers lead historically with 18 and 17 championships respectively.
- **Affiliated Properties:** **WNBA** (Women's National Basketball Association, est. 1996), **NBA G League** (official minor league developmental system).

#### EuroLeague & EuroCup
- **Governing Body:** Euroleague Basketball
- **Format:** Semi-closed pan-European league featuring 18 elite clubs from Spain, Greece, Turkey, Italy, Lithuania, France, Serbia, and Germany, concluding with the EuroLeague Final Four.
- **Significance:** Regarded as the second-strongest club competition in the world after the NBA, characterized by tactical half-court systems and intense atmospheres. Leading clubs include Real Madrid, Panathinaikos, CSKA Moscow, Maccabi Tel Aviv, and Olympiacos.

#### National Basketball League (NBL - Australia & New Zealand)
- **Founded:** 1979.
- **Geography:** Clubs across Australian states and Auckland, New Zealand (New Zealand Breakers).
- **Significance:** The premier professional league in Oceania, recognized as a primary development pathway to the NBA (Next Stars program). Includes the **WNBL** (Women's National Basketball League).

#### Top Domestic National Leagues
- **Liga ACB (Spain):** Widely recognized as Europe's premier domestic league outside the NBA.
- **BSL (Turkey), Serie A (Italy), LNB Élite (France), Basketball Bundesliga (Germany), Greek Basket League.**
- **Asian Leagues:** CBA (Chinese Basketball Association), B.League (Japan), KBL (Korean Basketball League), PBA (Philippine Basketball Association - the oldest professional basketball league in Asia, est. 1975).

## 3. Cricket (T20, Tests, One-Day Format)

---

### Cricket: Tests (`cricket tests`)

#### ICC World Test Championship (WTC)
- **Governing Body:** International Cricket Council (ICC)
- **Inaugural Edition:** 2019–2021 (Final at Southampton: New Zealand def. India).
- **Cycle & Format:** Two-year league cycle of bilateral Test series, culminating in a one-off final at a neutral venue (historically Lord's, The Oval, Rose Bowl in England). 2021–2023 cycle won by Australia; 2023–2025 cycle ongoing.
- **Trophy:** ICC Test Championship Mace.
- **Significance:** Created to provide a contextual narrative framework for five-day Test cricket, the traditional and purest format of the game.

#### Bilateral Test Series & The Ashes
- **The Ashes:** Celebrated biennial Test series between England and Australia, initiated in 1882 following an Australian victory at The Oval. Played alternatively in England and Australia across five Test matches.
- **Border-Gavaskar Trophy:** Australia vs. India.
- **Freedom Trophy / Gandhi-Mandela Series:** India vs. South Africa.
- **Anthony de Mello Trophy / Pataudi Trophy:** India vs. England.

#### First-Class Domestic Tournaments (`national/`)
- **Sheffield Shield (Australia):** Established 1892–93. Australia's premier six-state four-day first-class competition.
- **County Championship (England and Wales):** Established officially in 1890. Contested across 18 first-class county clubs divided into two divisions.
- **Ranji Trophy (India):** Established 1934. India's state-based first-class tournament (over 38 teams), the bedrock of Indian cricket talent.
- **Plunket Shield (New Zealand):** First contested 1906–07. Six major association teams.
- **Quaid-e-Azam Trophy (Pakistan) & Currie Cup / 4-Day Series (South Africa).**

### Cricket: One-Day Format (`cricket one-day format`)

#### Men's Cricket World Cup (50-over ODI)
- **Governing Body:** ICC
- **Inaugural Edition:** 1975 (England; Prudential Cup won by West Indies).
- **Cycle & Format:** Quadrennial 50-over tournament (60-over in 1975, 1979, 1983).
- **Trophy:** ICC Men's Cricket World Cup Trophy.
- **Most Successful Nations:** Australia (6 titles: 1987, 1999, 2003, 2007, 2015, 2023), India (2), West Indies (2), Pakistan (1), Sri Lanka (1), England (1).
- **Historical Hosts (Recent):**
  - 1987: India & Pakistan
  - 1992: Australia & New Zealand
  - 1996: India, Pakistan & Sri Lanka
  - 1999: England, Wales, Scotland, Ireland & Netherlands
  - 2003: South Africa, Zimbabwe & Kenya
  - 2007: West Indies
  - 2011: India, Sri Lanka & Bangladesh
  - 2015: Australia & New Zealand
  - 2019: England & Wales
  - 2023: India
  - 2027: South Africa, Zimbabwe & Namibia *(Upcoming)*

#### Women's Cricket World Cup
- **Governing Body:** ICC (IWCC prior to 2005)
- **Inaugural Edition:** 1973 (England — preceded the men's tournament by two years).
- **Dominant Nations:** Australia (7 titles), England (4), New Zealand (1).
- **Historical Hosts (Recent):** 1988: Australia; 1993: England; 1997: India; 2000: New Zealand; 2005: South Africa; 2009: Australia; 2013: India; 2017: England; 2022: New Zealand; 2025: India & Sri Lanka.

#### ICC Men's Champions Trophy
- **Inaugural Edition:** 1998 (Bangladesh, originally ICC KnockOut Tournament).
- **Format:** "Mini-World Cup" contested between the top eight ODI-ranked nations in a concise round-robin and knockout format.
- **Historical Hosts:** 1998: Bangladesh; 2000: Kenya; 2002: Sri Lanka; 2004: England; 2006: India; 2009: South Africa; 2013: England & Wales; 2017: England & Wales; 2025: Pakistan & UAE.

#### Domestic One-Day Tournaments (`national/`)
- **Marsh One-Day Cup (Australia):** State 50-over tournament.
- **Metro Bank One Day Cup (England):** 50-over county competition.
- **Vijay Hazare Trophy (India):** State-based 50-over competition.

### National & Club Competitions (`national/`)

#### National Rugby League (NRL)
- **Governing Body:** Australian Rugby League Commission (ARLC)
- **Inaugural Season:** 1998 (unification of ARL and Super League war; roots trace to NSWRL founded 1908).
- **Geography:** 17 clubs across New South Wales, Queensland, Victoria, Australian Capital Territory, and New Zealand (New Zealand Warriors).
- **Format:** 27-round regular season, top-8 McIntyre/finals system, concluding with the NRL Grand Final at Sydney's Accor Stadium.
- **Trophy:** Provan-Summons Trophy.
- **Affiliated League:** **NRL Women's Premiership (NRLW)**, established 2018.

#### State of Origin
- **Contested Between:** New South Wales Blues and Queensland Maroons.
- **Selection Rule:** Players are selected based on the state where they played their first senior rugby league match, creating ferocious tribal rivalry.
- **Format:** Annual best-of-three mid-season series played in Sydney, Brisbane, and neutral interstate venues (Melbourne, Perth, Adelaide). Widely considered the most intense, brutal, and highest-speed rugby league on earth.

#### Super League (Europe)
- **Founded:** 1996 (transition from winter to summer rugby).
- **Geography:** Top tier in the UK, historically featuring French clubs (Catalans Dragons, Toulouse Olympique).
- **Format:** 27 rounds concluding with the Super League Grand Final at Old Trafford, Manchester.
- **Lower Tiers:** RFL Championship and League 1.

## 5. Rugby Union

### International Competitions (`international/`)

#### Men's Rugby World Cup (RWC)
- **Governing Body:** World Rugby (formerly IRB)
- **Inaugural Edition:** 1987 (Australia & New Zealand; won by New Zealand).
- **Cycle & Format:** Quadrennial 20-team championship (expanding to 24 in 2027). Pool stage followed by quarter-finals, semi-finals, and final.
- **Trophy:** Webb Ellis Cup.
- **Most Successful Nations:** South Africa (4 titles: 1995, 2007, 2019, 2023), New Zealand (3: 1987, 2011, 2015), Australia (2: 1991, 1999), England (1: 2003).
- **Historical Hosts:**
  - 1987: New Zealand & Australia
  - 1991: England, France, Ireland, Scotland & Wales
  - 1995: South Africa
  - 1999: Wales (matches in England, France, Ireland, Scotland)
  - 2003: Australia
  - 2007: France (matches in Scotland, Wales)
  - 2011: New Zealand
  - 2015: England & Wales
  - 2019: Japan (first tournament in Asia)
  - 2023: France
  - 2027: Australia *(Upcoming)*
  - 2031: United States *(Upcoming)*

#### Women's Rugby World Cup
- **Inaugural Edition:** 1991 (Wales).
- **Dominant Teams:** New Zealand (Black Ferns - 6 titles), England (Red Roses - 2 titles).
- **Recent Hosts:** 2010: England; 2014: France; 2017: Ireland; 2021 (played 2022): New Zealand; 2025: England; 2029: Australia.

#### Annual International Tier 1 Tournaments
- **Six Nations Championship:** Annual European national team championship contested between England, France, Ireland, Italy, Scotland, and Wales. Roots trace to the Home Nations Championship (1883). Includes the Triple Crown and Grand Slam honors.
- **The Rugby Championship:** Southern Hemisphere annual tournament contested between New Zealand (All Blacks), South Africa (Springboks), Australia (Wallabies), and Argentina (Pumas). Formerly the Tri Nations (1996–2011). Contests the Bledisloe Cup and Freedom Cup.

### National & Club Competitions (`national/`)

#### Super Rugby Pacific
- **Governing Body:** SANZAAR
- **Founded:** 1996 (as Super 12; later Super 14, Super 15, and Super Rugby Pacific).
- **Geography:** Current format includes 12 franchises representing New Zealand, Australia, Fiji (Fijian Drua), and the Pacific Islands (Moana Pasifika). Historically included South African, Argentine, and Japanese sides.

#### European Professional Club Rugby
- **European Rugby Champions Cup (Investec Champions Cup):** The premier annual club tournament in northern hemisphere rugby. Features top clubs from England (Premiership), France (Top 14), and URC (Ireland, Scotland, Wales, Italy, South Africa).
- **United Rugby Championship (URC):** Multi-national cross-border league featuring 16 clubs from Ireland (Leinster, Munster, Ulster, Connacht), Scotland, Wales, Italy, and South Africa (Bulls, Stormers, Sharks, Lions).
- **Top 14 (France):** Renowned as the richest and most physically punishing domestic rugby league in the world. Concludes with the Bouclier de Brennus.
- **Gallagher Premiership (England):** England's top tier club league.
- **Japan Rugby League One:** Rapidly expanding professional league attracting world-class international stars.
- **Women's Club Rugby:** Premiership Women's Rugby (England) and Farah Palmer Cup (New Zealand provincial championship).

## 6. Ice Hockey

### International Competitions (`international/`)

#### Winter Olympic Ice Hockey Tournament
- **Governing Body:** IOC / International Ice Hockey Federation (IIHF)
- **Inaugural Edition:** Summer Olympics 1920 (Antwerp); moved permanently to Winter Olympics in 1924 (Chamonix). Women's tournament introduced in 1998 (Nagano).
- **Participation:** Features top international national teams. NHL players participated in 1998, 2002, 2006, 2010, 2014, and scheduled for 2026 in Milan-Cortina.
- **Recent Olympic Hosts:** 1992: France; 1994: Norway; 1998: Japan; 2002: USA; 2006: Italy; 2010: Canada; 2014: Russia; 2018: South Korea; 2022: China; 2026: Italy.

#### IIHF Men's & Women's World Championships
- **Governing Body:** IIHF
- **Cycle & Format:** Annual tournament contested each spring. Top division features 16 nations playing in two groups followed by single-elimination medal rounds.
- **Recent Hosts (Men's):** 2016: Russia; 2017: Germany & France; 2018: Denmark; 2019: Slovakia; 2021: Latvia; 2022: Finland; 2023: Finland & Latvia; 2024: Czechia; 2025: Sweden & Denmark; 2026: Switzerland.

---

### National & Club Competitions (`national/`)

#### National Hockey League (NHL)
- **Founded:** 1917 (Montreal, Quebec).
- **Geography:** 32 franchises across the United States (25) and Canada (7).
- **Format:** 82-game regular season, followed by the 16-team four-round best-of-seven Stanley Cup Playoffs.
- **Trophy:** The Stanley Cup — the oldest professional sports trophy in North America (first awarded 1893).
- **Affiliated Minor Leagues:** **AHL** (American Hockey League, primary development circuit) and **ECHL**.
- **Dominant Franchises:** Montreal Canadiens (24 Stanley Cups), Toronto Maple Leafs (13), Detroit Red Wings (11), Boston Bruins (6), Chicago Blackhawks (6).

#### Professional Women's Hockey League (PWHL)
- **Founded:** 2023 (first season 2024).
- **Geography:** 6 founding franchises across North America (Boston, Minnesota, Montreal, New York, Ottawa, Toronto).
- **Significance:** The unified, commercially sustainable top tier for professional women's ice hockey.

#### Top European Leagues & Australia
- **Kontinental Hockey League (KHL):** Premier league in Eurasia/Russia, awarding the Gagarin Cup.
- **Swedish Hockey League (SHL), Liiga (Finland), Deutsche Eishockey Liga (DEL - Germany), National League (NL - Switzerland), Czech Extraliga (ELH).**
- **Australian Ice Hockey League (AIHL):** Contested across Australian cities for the Goodall Cup (first awarded 1909).

## 7. Field Hockey (`hockey`)

### International Competitions (`international/`)
- **Summer Olympic Field Hockey Tournament:** Men's debut in 1908; Women's in 1980. Traditionally dominated by India (8 historical golds) and Pakistan in early decades, followed by modern dominance from the Netherlands, Germany, Australia (Kookaburras/Hockeyroos), Belgium, and Argentina.
- **FIH Hockey World Cup:** Quadrennial global championship organized by the International Hockey Federation (FIH). Founded 1971 (Men's) and 1974 (Women's).
- **FIH Hockey Pro League:** Global annual home-and-away league for top national teams.

### National & Club Competitions (`national/`)
- **Euro Hockey League (EHL):** The top European club field hockey tournament.
- **Hoofdklasse (Netherlands):** Widely regarded as the strongest domestic field hockey club league in the world.
- **Hockey India League (HIL):** Franchise tournament reviving professional club hockey in India.

## 8. Tennis (Lawn Tennis & Claycourt Tennis)

---

### Lawn Tennis (`lawn tennis`)

#### The Championships, Wimbledon
- **Governing Body / Venue:** All England Lawn Tennis and Croquet Club (AELTC), London, United Kingdom.
- **Founded:** 1877 — The oldest and most prestigious tennis tournament in the world.
- **Surface:** 100% Perennial Ryegrass (mowed to exactly 8 mm).
- **Traditions:** Strict all-white dress code, royal patronage, consumption of strawberries and cream, lack of sponsor perimeter boards.
- **Format:** 128-player men's and women's singles draws over two weeks (best-of-five sets for men, best-of-three for women).
- **Significance:** The crown jewel of the grass-court season, characterized by fast ball speed, low skid bounces, and attacking serve-and-volley play.

#### Warm-Up Grass Tournaments (`national/`)
- **Queen's Club Championships (cinch Championships, London):** ATP 500.
- **Halle Open (Terra Wortmann Open, Germany):** ATP 500.
- **Eastbourne International & Bad Homburg Open.**

---

### Claycourt Tennis (`claycourt tennis`)

#### French Open (Roland-Garros)
- **Governing Body / Venue:** Fédération Française de Tennis (FFT), Stade Roland Garros, Paris, France.
- **Founded:** 1891 (opened to international entrants in 1925).
- **Surface:** Crushed white limestone topped with red brick dust (red clay).
- **Format:** 128-player singles draws; physically demanding baseline battles over five sets.
- **Trophies:** Coupe des Mousquetaires (Men's), Coupe Suzanne Lenglen (Women's).
- **Record Performance:** Rafael Nadal's historic dominance (14 titles between 2005 and 2022).
- **Significance:** The supreme test of physical endurance, spin generation, foot sliding, and patience, as clay slows the ball down and produces high bounces.

#### European Clay Masters & Circuit Tournaments (`national/`)
- **Monte-Carlo Masters:** ATP Masters 1000.
- **Madrid Open (Mutua Madrid Open):** ATP Masters 1000 / WTA 1000 (high-altitude clay).
- **Italian Open (Internazionali BNL d'Italia, Rome):** ATP Masters 1000 / WTA 1000.
- **Barcelona Open (Trofeo Conde de Godó):** ATP 500.

## 9. Baseball

#### World Baseball Classic (WBC)
- **Sanctioning Body:** World Baseball Softball Confederation (WBSC) & Major League Baseball (MLB / MLBPA).

#### Olympic Baseball / Softball & WBSC Premier12

### National & Club Competitions (`national/`)

#### Major League Baseball (MLB)
- **Founded:** 1876 (National League) & 1901 (American League); unified organization in 2000.
- **Geography:** 30 clubs across the USA (29) and Canada (1 — Toronto Blue Jays).
- **Format:** 162-game regular season across AL and NL, followed by Wild Card, Division Series, League Championship Series, and the World Series.

## 10. Australian Rules Football (`afl` & `state level afl`)

---

### Australian Football League (`afl`)

#### AFL Premiership
- **Governing Body:** AFL Commission
- **Inaugural Season:** 1897 (founded as Victorian Football League, VFL; national expansion prompted renaming to AFL in 1990).
- **Geography:** 18 clubs across five Australian states (Victoria: 10, South Australia: 2, Western Australia: 2, Queensland: 2, New South Wales: 2).

### State Level Australian Rules Football (`state level afl`)

#### Historical State Competitions
Before the VFL's national expansion in the late 1980s, Australian rules football was administered by powerful sovereign state leagues with rich traditions:
- **Victorian Football League / VFL (formerly VFA):** Victoria's primary reserves and standalone club league.

## 11. American Football

#### National Football League (NFL)

## 12. College Football (`college football`)

### NCAA Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS)

#### Structure & Divisions
- **Governing Body:** National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA)
- **Inaugural Game:** November 6, 1869 (Rutgers vs. Princeton).
- **Organization:** Contested by major university athletic departments divided into conferences:
  - **Power Four Conferences:** Southeastern Conference (SEC), Big Ten Conference, Atlantic Coast Conference (ACC), Big 12 Conference.
  - **Group of Five Conferences:** American Athletic (AAC), Mountain West (MWC), Sun Belt, Mid-American (MAC), Conference USA (CUSA).

#### Championship Systems
- **Historical Era (1869–1997):** National championships were awarded by pollsters (Associated Press Poll, Coaches' Poll) without a unified title game.
- **Bowl Championship Series (BCS, 1998–2013):** Algorithmic and poll formula matching the #1 and #2 ranked teams in the BCS National Championship Game.
- **College Football Playoff (CFP, 2014–2023):** 4-team playoff selected by a 13-member selection committee.
- **Expanded CFP (2024–Present):** 12-team playoff featuring top conference champions and at-large seeds.
- **Trophy:** College Football Playoff National Championship Trophy.

#### Historic Bowl Games
- **The Rose Bowl ("The Granddaddy of Them All", Pasadena, CA):** First played 1902.
- **The Sugar Bowl (New Orleans, LA):** First played 1935.
- **The Orange Bowl (Miami, FL):** First played 1935.
- **The Cotton Bowl Classic (Dallas/Arlington, TX):** First played 1937.
- **Fiesta Bowl (Glendale, AZ) & Peach Bowl (Atlanta, GA).**

#### NCAA Division I FCS & Lower Divisions
- **FCS Championship:** Single-elimination 24-team tournament concluding with the FCS National Championship game in Frisco, Texas (dominated by North Dakota State University and South Dakota State).

## 13. Canadian Football (`canadian football`)

### Canadian Football League (CFL)
- **Governing Body:** Canadian Football League Board of Governors
- **Inaugural Season:** Officially founded 1958 (roots trace to the Canadian Rugby Football Union founded 1884).
- **Geography:** 9 franchises divided into East Division (Hamilton Tiger-Cats, Montreal Alouettes, Ottawa Redblacks, Toronto Argonauts) and West Division (BC Lions, Calgary Stampeders, Edmonton Elks, Saskatchewan Roughriders, Winnipeg Blue Bombers).
- **Unique Rules vs. American Football:**
  - 12 players per side (instead of 11).
  - 3 downs to make 10 yards (instead of 4).
  - Field dimensions: 110 yards long by 65 yards wide, with 20-yard end zones and goalposts on the goal line.
  - Unlimited offensive backfield motion toward the line of scrimmage before the snap (*the waggle*).
  - The Single point (*rouge*), awarded when a kicked ball enters the end zone and is not returned.
- **Trophy:** **The Grey Cup** — commissioned by Governor General Albert Grey in 1909. It is the second-oldest trophy in North American professional sport after the Stanley Cup.
- **Record Winner:** Toronto Argonauts (18+ Grey Cup championships).

### Canadian University Football
- **U Sports Football:** Contested across Canadian universities (OUA, RSEQ, Canada West, AUS), concluding with the **Vanier Cup**.
