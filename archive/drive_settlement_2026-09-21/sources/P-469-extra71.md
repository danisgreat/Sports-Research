 Hawthorn fans rain bottles on umpires after contentious call as Brisbane claims prelim thriller

 By Chris De Silva
 Topic: AFL

 Furious Hawthorn fans have thrown bottles at the field umpires after Brisbane benefited from a massive late call in their preliminary final epic at the MCG.
 After the Hawks had recovered from a 28-point deficit in the early stages of the final quarter, Hugh McCluggage put the Lions back in front from a set shot with 18 seconds remaining on the clock, before Kai Lohmann goalled after the siren to give Brisbane a 20.11 (131) to 19.8 (122) win in front of 97,835 spectators. 
 McCluggage was awarded a set shot on goal from 30m out on a slight angle after Hawthorn defender Jarman Impey was penalised for holding the ball , a decision that was greeted by deafening boos from the Hawthorn faithful.
 Loading Twitter content 
 The decision, while controversial, appeared to be the correct one, with Impey&#x27;s left arm free to dispose of the football while being tackled.
 "I think this might be the bravest umpiring decision I&#x27;ve seen in my time in footy," Melbourne great Garry Lyon said on Fox Footy&#x27;s post-game coverage.
 McCluggage said he knew the umpires would call holding the ball on Impey after he pinned a single arm.
 "I tried to grab a lever [arm] like we&#x27;re taught," he told Fox Footy.
 McCluggage (6) put Brisbane in front after the free kick was awarded against Impey. ( Getty Images: James Wiltshire/AFL Photos ) 
 "We&#x27;ve been taught that you have to try and make an effort to get the ball out if you&#x27;ve got an arm free, so I&#x27;ll take it."
 Hawthorn coach Sam Mitchell was left deeply emotional, and said his focus was on other umpiring calls, particularly a deliberate out of bounds call against Mitch Lewis and a high contact free kick to Dayne Zorko. 
 "It&#x27;s probably not the one I&#x27;m focused on. There were a couple leading up to it," Mitchell said when asked about the Impey call.
 "I&#x27;m sure the umpires will assess how they performed in that game in key moments."
 Loading Twitter content 
 The final moments led to ugly scenes after the match, and Mitchell joined former teammate Luke Hodge in condemning the behaviour from the fans.
 "The emotion of sport is why we love it so much," he said.
 "At the end of the game, part of me wanted to break down in tears, part of me wanted to scream with rage, part of me wanted to throw chairs.
 "When you&#x27;re in the stands, you need to continue to be feeling that emotion, because that&#x27;s why we love it, but we shouldn&#x27;t be throwing things on the field. But the passion of the game is why we love it.
 "I hope for the vast majority that it was done responsibly."
 Watson was outstanding for Hawthorn in the loss, finishing with a game-high six goals. ( Getty Images: James Wiltshire/AFL Photos ) 
 Nick Watson was outstanding for Hawthorn in the loss, finishing with six goals, while Jack Gunston and Mitch Lewis combined for nine goals between them.
 Lohmann&#x27;s five goals led the way for the Lions, while Eric Hipwood, McCluggage and Charlie Cameron kicked three goals apiece. 
 Brisbane&#x27;s win means it will contest Fremantle in next weekend&#x27;s grand final with the hope a second three-peat in club history after doing so between 2001 and 2003 under Leigh Matthews.
 Brisbane veteran Lachie Neale will play against his old side after playing in Fremantle&#x27;s last grand final appearance in 2013 as a 20-year-old.
