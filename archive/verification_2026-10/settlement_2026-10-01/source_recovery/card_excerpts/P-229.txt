What went right: the research found almost every decisive mechanism. KT was 66-43-3 against Hanwha's 49-62-3, led the H2H 8-3, had the stronger/rested bullpen, returned Sam Hilliard, and faced a Hanwha team on a six-game losing streak. The card explicitly described KT's 5-2/6-2 low-total separation branch—the realized 6-1 score was nearly identical.

Why the “most likely” pick failed: Hanwha +1.5 was elevated mainly because it was the shortest-priced and broadest mathematical contract. That ignored the evidence's direction and severity. The log itself recorded Hanwha +1.5 at **0/5 and 5/20**, KT -1.5 at **7/11 H2Hs**, and a major late-inning edge for KT. A contract cannot be called the most robust merely because it covers one-run outcomes when the matchup repeatedly produces multi-run KT wins.

Why rank #2 failed: the Over leaned heavily on 9/11 H2H Overs and team season scoring, but the current starter state pointed Under: Daniel had a 2.81 ERA, White was much better than Hanwha's team ERA, and KT's latest ten were 8/10 Under. Required improvement:

1. Treat market price as a baseline, not a veto over strong matchup evidence. Document when the model view disagrees with price instead of mechanically ranking the shortest price first.
2. Add a contradiction penalty: a pick opposed by L5, L20, direct H2H, winner direction and bullpen direction cannot rank first without a specific new causal change.
3. Score one-sided totals. A weak bullpen can help KT separation while the game remains Under if Hanwha scores only 0–2.
4. The evidence-consistent order should have been **KT -1.5 / Under 9.5 / Hanwha +1.5 / Over 9.5**. The original analysis contained that answer but the ranking did not follow it.
