## P-229 settlement and deep rank-#1 retrospective — Hanwha @ KT

Verified outcome: **KT 6, Hanwha 1; 7 total runs; KT won by five.**

| Rank | Issued pick | Grade |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Hanwha +1.5 | **LOSS** |
| 2 | Over 9.5 | **LOSS** |
| 3 | Under 9.5 | **WIN** |
| 4 | KT -1.5 | **WIN** |
| — | Potential winner: KT | **WIN** |

What went right: the research found almost every decisive mechanism. KT was 66-43-3 against Hanwha's 49-62-3, led the H2H 8-3, had the stronger/rested bullpen, returned Sam Hilliard, and faced a Hanwha team on a six-game losing streak. The card explicitly described KT's 5-2/6-2 low-total separation branch—the realized 6-1 score was nearly identical.

Why the “most likely” pick failed: Hanwha +1.5 was elevated mainly because it was the shortest-priced and broadest mathematical contract. That ignored the evidence's direction and severity. The log itself recorded Hanwha +1.5 at **0/5 and 5/20**, KT -1.5 at **7/11 H2Hs**, and a major late-inning edge for KT. A contract cannot be called the most robust merely because it covers one-run outcomes when the matchup repeatedly produces multi-run KT wins.

Why rank #2 failed: the Over leaned heavily on 9/11 H2H Overs and team season scoring, but the current starter state pointed Under: Daniel had a 2.81 ERA, White was much better than Hanwha's team ERA, and KT's latest ten were 8/10 Under. Once KT controlled Hanwha, the weak Hanwha offense could not contribute enough to reach 10.

Required improvement:

1. Treat market price as a baseline, not a veto over strong matchup evidence. Document when the model view disagrees with price instead of mechanically ranking the shortest price first.
2. Add a contradiction penalty: a pick opposed by L5, L20, direct H2H, winner direction and bullpen direction cannot rank first without a specific new causal change.
3. Score one-sided totals. A weak bullpen can help KT separation while the game remains Under if Hanwha scores only 0–2.
4. The evidence-consistent order should have been **KT -1.5 / Under 9.5 / Hanwha +1.5 / Over 9.5**. The original analysis contained that answer but the ranking did not follow it.

| Rank | Issued pick | Grade |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Doosan +1.5 | **LOSS** |
| 2 | Under 8.5 | **WIN** |
| 3 | LG +0.5 | **WIN** |
| 4 | Over 8.5 | **LOSS** |
| — | Potential winner: LG | **WIN** |

What went right: the starter/H2H/park analysis was strong. Im Chan-kyu's current form and 2.04 ERA versus Doosan, Logue's credible but weaker state, Jamsil's run suppression and a user total above the market centre all pointed to LG plus Under. The winner, Under and LG +0.5 all won.

Why rank #1 failed: Doosan +1.5 was promoted on recent cover counts (18/20) and contract breadth even though the best game-specific evidence pointed to a low-scoring LG win. At low totals, a two-run margin is not rare: 3-1, 4-2 and 5-3 are natural results. The exact 3-1 final was already represented by the original “LG controlled separation” logic, yet that branch was not weighted enough.

This was a threshold failure by the minimum margin: Doosan +1.5 needed a one-run loss and lost by two. That does not make the underlying close-game logic absurd, but it does show the ranking was too confident relative to the line's sensitivity.

Required improvement:

1. For +1.5 at a low total, measure the probability of the favourite winning by exactly two; do not bundle all low-score outcomes into “close.”
2. Use opponent-adjusted cover windows. Doosan's 18/20 record was not conditioned on facing the hottest starter in the matchup.
3. When winner + starter + Under all align, elevate the winner-aligned side above the opposing cushion unless the dog has a clear scoring floor.
4. A better order was **Under 8.5 / LG +0.5 / Doosan +1.5 / Over 8.5**.
