 Fremantle through to first AFL grand final since 2013 after stunning final-quarter comeback

 By Chris De Silva

 Fremantle has pulled off a sensational last-quarter comeback to surge into the AFL grand final with a tense 12-point win over Sydney.
 Trailing by 27 points late in the third quarter, the Dockers kicked the last six goals of their preliminary final to win 12.11 (83) to 10.11 (71) at the SCG.
 Fremantle advances to the second grand final in club history, where they will meet either Hawthorn or Brisbane.
 Justin Longmuir&#x27;s men were staring down the barrel of an early exit before Hayden Young converted a difficult set shot after the three-quarter time siren, and were full of running in a gripping finish.
 Loading Twitter content 
 Young (28 disposals), Andrew Brayshaw (29), Caleb Serong (28) and Shai Bolton (two goals) were instrumental in the victory.
 But it wasn&#x27;t assured until the dying stages, when Isaiah Dudley ran into an open goal with less than two minutes on the clock.
 "Words can&#x27;t describe how proud I am of everyone for stepping up," Fremantle captain Alex Pearce told Channel 7.
 "There were just so many moments where guys stood up under the fiercest of pressure.
 " We played our best footy in the last quarter when everything was against us. " 
 Luke Jackson finished with two goals in an entertaining ruck battle with Brodie Grundy.
 Dudley&#x27;s second major was the Dockers&#x27; fifth of a dominant final quarter, in which Patrick Voss&#x27;s huge fend-off and checkside goal on the run was a highlight.
 "[Our forwards] were beaten early, and it was hurting us a little bit," Pearce said.
 "Our ball use into the forward line was diabolical at times. We were just kicking it to the red jumper.
 "But wow, our forwards stood up [in the final quarter]. They stood up like big men."
 Loading Twitter content 
 Sydney&#x27;s brave effort came without injured captain Callum Mills and the five stars banned in the fallout from the Pullman Hotel scandal.
 Errol Gulden (36 disposals) was brilliant in setting up their lead, with Logan McDonald (three goals) dangerous in attack.
 "It&#x27;s gut-wrenching, devastating, but I&#x27;m overwhelmed with pride in what we&#x27;ve been able to do over the last couple weeks," Swans stand-in captain Dane Rampe said.
 "We didn&#x27;t get that result tonight, but I felt over the last couple of weeks we took a couple of steps in the right direction towards [getting] back respect and trust of not only our fans and our club, but the AFL community in general."
 Swans coach appeared emotional during his post-match press conference, but spoke of his pride in the group.
 "I&#x27;m extremely proud. To put on a performance like that, I&#x27;m just extremely proud of everyone uniting and galvanising to get our season to the last game, and we fell short," Cox said.
 "It is raw, but that is footy, footy is a hard game. I learnt a hell of a lot. I&#x27;ve got a chance to really reflect on the season we&#x27;ve had.
 "We want to be playing these games of footy every year, that&#x27;s what we strive for. I love prelim finals.
 "We&#x27;ve got to learn, and I do as well, and we&#x27;ll spend the off-season doing that. They&#x27;ve been really supportive and wanted to action everything we wanted to put in place. They&#x27;ve just been really hungry to improve.
 "It&#x27;s hard to say it&#x27;s a missed opportunity when you play a really good footy team."
