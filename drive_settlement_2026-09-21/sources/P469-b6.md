 Lions prevail in all-time classic after decisive late call denies Hawks
 Brisbane advances to the Grand Final with a nine-point win over Hawthorn
 By Sarah Black 
 -->
 Kai Lohmann celebrates kicking a goal during the preliminary final between Hawthorn and Brisbane at the MCG on September 19, 2026. Picture: Getty Images 
 BRISBANE is into its fourth straight Grand Final after an absolute classic of a preliminary final, defeating Hawthorn by nine points in a match that will go straight into the history books.
 Co-captain Hugh McCluggage kicked truly after Jarman Impey was pinged – perhaps harshly – for holding the ball in front of goal with just 36 seconds left, and Brisbane hit the lead.
 Kai Lohmann's fifth goal, coming after the siren, sewed up the 20.11 (131) to 19.8 (122) triumph, which saw 12 majors kicked in the final term between the two sides.
 HAWKS v LIONS Full match coverage and stats 
 It was a true heavyweight bout, dominated by the small forwards, with Nick Watson doing all in his power to drag Hawthorn over the line.
 Brisbane had led by as much as 28 points in the fourth term, but Hawthorn had absolutely nothing to lose, and weren't going to die wondering.

 They say the preliminary finals are often louder than the Grand Finals, and it was certainly the case as 97,835 people at the MCG completely and utterly lost their minds, with the most human and primal wall of noise that greeted Hawthorn's comeback. 
 Mabior Chol and Jack Gunston dragged the margin back to an eminently gettable 16 points, Impey brought down Charlie Cameron with every inch of desperation he could muster, and the Wizard, the Wizard, the Wizard … Watson, the shortest man on the field, took the Hawks on his back and booted two goals – his fifth and sixth – to snatch back the lead.

 Both sides threw punches throughout that would have knocked out lesser sides, but given both the stakes and the talent on show, everyone kept coming back for more.
 Ryan Lester was initially charged with the toughest role in football – trying to keep a tight rein on Watson – but the Wizard set the tone from the first minute, powering to the front of the pack and soccering truly, before taking advantage of a free for holding.

 Each Watson major was quickly met by a Brisbane goal, and there was no chance the Lions were going to be caught napping like Fremantle was against the Hawks a few weeks ago.
 The goalkicking in the first term from both teams was immaculate, with Lewis, Will Ashcroft, Eric Hipwood and Conor McKenna all pulling out absolute pearlers, whether it was a set shot, on the run, around the body or the most precise of soccer strikes, fans were treated to an absolute exhibition.

 But the issue with a team pulling goals out of its proverbial is that it's very hard to keep up over four quarters, and without a steady, consistent supply to attack, the Lions' scoring began to dry up.
 Hawthorn's tall defenders – and floating ruck Lloyd Meek – stood up well under the high ball, which Brisbane bafflingly continued to send in their direction throughout most of the second term.
 At the same time, the Hawks' ground brigade was using the ball smartly, whether it was by conventional disposal or taps, bumps, flicks or spoils, managing to match the Lions' much-vaunted and deep midfield.
 FEATURE
 Kai-boom: How Lion went from bystander to firestarter in a flash 
 Mistakes appeared to be compounding for Brisbane. Darcy Fort had a bizarre brain-fade and attempted to drag Josh Weddle out of a ruck contest, and the Hawks were up by 22.
 But they're back-to-back premiers for a reason. Kai Lohmann – barely sighted to that point – was the beneficiary of a generous free kick and capitalised, before Cameron's third (another addition to the highlight reel) and the Lions were back in action.

 The champions began to flex their muscles.
 Harris Andrews patrolled the backline with authority. Charlie Cameron twisted and turned and won crucial contests. The much-maligned Eric Hipwood clunked everything that came his way. Josh Dunkley doesn't appear to bash and crash, but he certainly won more than his fair share of centre clearances.

 POST-MATCH
 'An OK decision': Fagan backs huge late call as Lions make GF 
 Cobra Kai and the Wizard
 If the first half was all about Nick Watson, then the second was the time for Brisbane's livewire forward to shine, the equally "love him if he was on your team" Kai Lohmann. He moved with authority through the forward line, commanding space, demanding the ball, and kicking truly to turn the game on its head in the third term with three of his own. Watson appeared to have won the game for the Hawks in the final quarter, but it was Lohmann who had the last laugh.
