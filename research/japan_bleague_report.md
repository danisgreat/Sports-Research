## 1. Executive Summary & Historical Chronology Overview

Japanese top-tier basketball over the past 50 years (1975–2025) has evolved through distinct evolutionary eras, marked by corporate amateur leagues, professional breakaways, FIBA suspension, and unification into one of Asia's top professional basketball leagues (B.League):

```
1967 ─── 1978 ────────────── 2000 ────── 2001 ────────── 2005 ────────── 2007 ────────── 2013 ────────── 2016 ──────────── 2026+
  │        │                   │           │               │               │               │               │                │
  └─ Japan League (日本リーグ) ─┘           │               │               │               │               │                │
     Div 1 / Div 2                         │               ├─ bj-league ───┼───────────────┼───────────────┤                │
     (1988-2000: Cougar/Tiger Divs)        │               │  (2005-2016)  │               │               │                │
                                           │               │               │               │               │                │
                                           ├─ JBL Super ───┘               ├─ JBL ─────────┼─ NBL Japan ───┤                │
                                           │  League (2001-07)             │  (2007-13)    │  (2013-16)    │                │
                                           │                               │  + JBL2       │  + NBDL       │                │
                                           │                               │               │               │                │
                                           └───────────────────────────────┴───────────────┴───────────────┴─ B.LEAGUE ────┴─ B.PREMIER
                                                                                                              (B1, B2, B3)      (New B)
```

## 2. Detailed Breakdown of Historical Eras & League Structures

### Era 1: Japan Basketball League / Japan League (日本リーグ, 1967–2000)
* **Background & Governance**: Founded in 1967 as the *All-Japan Corporate Basketball League* (全国実業団バスケットボールリーグ) under the Japan Basketball Association (JBA) and Japan Corporate Basketball Federation. Rebranded in 1970 as the *Basketball Japan League* (バスケットボール日本リーグ).
* **Division Structure & Evolution**:
  * **1971–1977**: Two-tier system with Japan League (Division 1) and Corporate League (Division 2). Division 1 had 8 corporate teams.
  * **1978–1987**: Corporate League absorbed to officially establish **Division 1 (1部)** and **Division 2 (2部)** under the Japan League banner.
  * **1988–2000 (Two-Division System)**: Division 1 expanded to 12 teams and split into two conferences:
    * Men: **Cougar Division (クーガー)** and **Tiger Division (タイガー)** (6 teams each).
    * Women: Andromeda and Cassiopeia Divisions (until WJBL split in 1998).
    * Division assignments were redrawn annually by lottery. Top 4 from each division advanced to the post-season championship tournament.
  * **Promotion / Relegation (入れ替え戦)**: Bottom 2 teams in Division 1 played the top 2 teams in Division 2 in a best-of-3 series. Bottom 2 of Division 2 played the top 2 of the All-Japan Corporate Championship.
  * **1995 JBL Organization**: Formation of the *Basketball Japan League Organization* (バスケットボール日本リーグ機構). Introduced "FRIDAY FUNKY NIGHT GAME" at Yoyogi 2nd Gymnasium and pre-season *JBL Tournaments* (1996, 1997).
* **Match Count & Rules**:
  * 1970–1981, 1983–1987: 2 round-robins (14 regular-season games per team).
  * 1982: Experimental 3 round-robins (21 games per team).
  * 1988–2000: 15–16 games per team in regular season + playoff knockout games.
  * Rule note: Played under older FIBA rules (two 20-minute halves) until FIBA transitioned to four 10-minute quarters in 2000.

### Era 2: JBL Super League (JBLスーパーリーグ, 2001–2007)
* **Structure & Format**:
  * 2000–01 ran as the *JBL Pre-Super League* (8 teams, 21 regular-season games).
  * 2001–02 through 2006–07: Division 1 rebranded as the **JBL Super League**, while Division 2 became the subordinate "Japan League" (日本リーグ).
  * 8 teams: Toyota Alvark, Aisin Seahorse, Toshiba Brave Thunders, Isuzu Motors Giga Cats (folded 2002), Matsushita Electric / Panasonic Trians, Hitachi Sunrockers, Mitsubishi Electric Diamond Dolphins, Bosch Blue Winds (folded 2002), OSG Phoenix (joined 2002), Niigata Albirex (joined 2002 before defecting to bj-league in 2005).
* **Match Counts**:
  * 2001–02 to 2002–03: 3 round-robins (21 games/team). Top 4 to playoffs (SF best-of-3, Final best-of-3).
  * 2003–04 to 2004–05: 4 round-robins (28 games/team). Semifinals best-of-3; Finals best-of-3 (2004) or best-of-5 (2005).
  * 2005–06: 26 games/team. Finals best-of-5.
  * 2006–07: 24 games/team. Finals best-of-5.
* **Promotion/Relegation**: Closed league with no direct sporting promotion/relegation between Super League and the lower Japan League.

### Era 3: Japan Basketball League (JBL, 2007–2013)
* **Structure**: Reorganized under JBA in 2007 into the new JBL (新JBL, Tier 1) and JBL2 (Tier 2).
* **Teams (8 Teams)**: Aisin Seahorse, Toyota Alvark, Toshiba Brave Thunders, Hitachi Sunrockers, Mitsubishi Electric Diamond Dolphins, Panasonic Trians, Rera Kamuy Hokkaido / Levanga Hokkaido (from 2007), Link Tochigi Brex (promoted from JBL2 in 2008), OSG Phoenix East Mikawa (2007–08 only, defected to bj-league in 2008).
* **Match Counts**:
  * 2007–08 & 2008–09: 5 round-robins = **35 games per team** (140 league games total). Playoffs: Top 4 (SF best-of-3, Final best-of-5).
  * 2009–10: 6 round-robins = **42 games per team** (168 league games). Top 4 playoffs (Link Tochigi Brex champion, MVP Yuta Tabuse).
  * 2010–11: 42 games scheduled. **Interrupted/cancelled on March 11, 2011 due to the Great East Japan Earthquake**. Remaining regular season games and all playoffs cancelled. Standings frozen by winning percentage (Aisin awarded 1st).
  * 2011–12 & 2012–13: 6 round-robins = **42 games per team** (168 league games total). Playoffs: SF best-of-3, Final best-of-5.
* **JBL2 (Tier 2)**: 8 teams (2007–2012), expanded to 10–12 teams (2012–13). Toyota Tsusho Fighting Eagles, Hitachi Cable, Kuroda Electric, Renova Kagoshima, Ishikawa, etc.

### Era 4: bj-league (Basketball Japan League / 日本プロバスケットボールリーグ, 2005–2016)
* **Background**: First independent professional basketball league in Japan, founded by Niigata Albirex and Saitama Broncos after frustration with JBA's corporate amateurism. Operated outside JBA jurisdiction (leading to severe friction with FIBA).
* **Growth & Conference Expansion**:
  * 2005–06: 6 teams (Niigata, Saitama, Sendai, Tokyo Apache, Osaka, Oita). Single table, **40 games/team** (120 total). Final Four at Ariake Colosseum.
  * 2006–07: 8 teams (+ Toyama, Takamatsu). Single table, **40 games/team** (160 total).
  * 2007–08: 10 teams (+ Fukuoka, Ryukyu). Split into Eastern and Western Conferences. **44 games/team** (220 total).
  * 2008–09: 12 teams (+ Hamamatsu, Shiga). **52 games/team** (312 total).
  * 2009–10: 13 teams (+ Kyoto). **52 games/team** (338 total).
  * 2010–11: 16 teams (+ Akita, Shimane, Miyazaki). **52 games scheduled** (Sendai, Saitama, Tokyo Apache games suspended mid-season due to earthquake; Saitama returned).
  * 2011–12: 19 teams (+ Iwate, Chiba Jets, Yokohama, Shinshu; Tokyo Apache ceased ops). **52 games/team**.
  * 2012–13: 21 teams (+ Gunma, Tokyo Cinq Rêves). **52 games/team**.
  * 2013–14: 21 teams (+ Aomori, Nara; Chiba transferred to NBL, Miyazaki dissolved). **52 games/team**.
  * 2014–15: 22 teams (+ Fukushima Firebonds). **52 games/team**.
  * 2015–16: 24 teams (+ Kanazawa Samuraiz, Hiroshima Lightning). **52 games/team** (624 regular season games total).
* **Playoff Structure**: Conference First Round (best-of-2 + 10-minute mini-game overtime if 1-1), Conference Semifinals (best-of-2 + mini-game), and the **Final Four at Ariake Colosseum** (Single elimination: Conf Finals, 3rd place game, Championship Final).

### Era 5: National Basketball League (NBL Japan / NBDL, 2013–2016)
* **Context**: JBA's attempt to restructure JBL into a pro/corporate hybrid ahead of the 2020 Olympics. Failure to integrate bj-league led FIBA to suspend the JBA in November 2014, barring Japan from international competition.
* **Format & Match Counts**:
  * **2013–14 & 2014–15**: 12 teams divided into Eastern and Western Conferences (6 teams each):
    * Intra-conference: 6 round-robins (30 games)
    * Inter-conference: 4 round-robins (24 games)
    * Total: **54 regular season games per team** (324 games total per season).
    * Playoffs: Top 3 per conference. Quarterfinals (best-of-3), Semifinals (best-of-3), Finals (best-of-5).
  * **2015–16**: 12 teams. Conferences abolished into a single unified table.
    * 5 round-robins = **55 regular season games per team** (330 total games).
    * Playoffs: Top 8 teams advance. Quarterfinals (best-of-3), Semifinals (best-of-3), Finals (best-of-5). Champion: Toshiba Brave Thunders Kanagawa.
* **NBDL (National Basketball Development League, Tier 2)**: 9–10 teams (corporate and developing pro clubs). 32–36 games per season. Tokyo Excellence won all three championships (2014, 2015, 2016).

### Era 6: B.LEAGUE (2016–17 to Present 2024–25 / 2025–26)
* **Formation**: Brokered by FIBA's Japan Taskforce led by Saburo Kawabuchi. United all NBL, NBDL, and bj-league teams into a 3-tier pyramid with sporting promotion and relegation.
* **Division Hierarchy**:
  * **B1 (First Division)**: Top professional tier.
  * **B2 (Second Division)**: Fully professional second tier.
  * **B3 (Third Division)**: De-facto semi-pro / developing pro league run by Japan Basketball Association / B3 League Association.
* **Season-by-Season Match Counts & Structure**:
  * **2016–17**: 18 teams (3 Conf: East, Central, West - 6 each). **60 games/team** (540 games). Championship: Top 8 (QF & SF best-of-2 + mini-game; Final single game at Yoyogi 1st Gym; Tochigi Brex champion). B1 Relegation Playoffs & B1/B2, B2/B3 Promotion/Relegation Series.
  * **2017–18 & 2018–19**: 18 teams (3 Conf - 6 each). **60 games/team**. Championship: Final single game at Yokohama Arena (Alvark Tokyo back-to-back champions).
  * **2019–20**: 18 teams. **COVID-19 suspended in March 2020** after ~40–41 games per team. Playoffs cancelled; no relegation; top 2 from B2 promoted.
  * **2020–21**: 20 teams (2 Conf: East 10, West 10). **60 games/team** (600 games). Championship expanded to best-of-3 for all rounds (QF, SF, Final). No relegation due to pandemic adjustments.
  * **2021–22**: 22 teams (2 Conf: East 11, West 11). **60 games/team** (660 games). Relegation reinstated. Utsunomiya Brex champion.
  * **2022–23 to 2024–25**: 24 teams (3 Conf: East 8, Central 8, West 8). **60 games per team** (720 regular season games total per season). Top 8 Championship playoffs (QF, SF, Final all best-of-3). Bottom 2 teams relegated to B2.
* **Upcoming 2026+ Reform ("B.革新" / B.Revolution)**: Transition to **B.PREMIER**, **B.ONE**, and **B.NEXT** based on business metrics (5,000+ seat arenas, ¥1.2B revenue, 4,000+ avg attendance) without sporting relegation.

### Era 7: Emperor's Cup (天皇杯・皇后杯 全日本バスケットボール選手権大会)
* **History**: Began in 1921. Known as "All Japan" / *全日本総合バスケットボール選手権大会* (All-Japan General Basketball Championship) from 1947 to 2016.
* **Pre-2016 Tournament Structure**: Knockout tournament conducted over ~7–10 days in early January in Tokyo (Yoyogi, Komazawa, Tokyo Metropolitan Gym). Included top corporate (JBL/NBL) clubs, university champion/runners-up, high school champion, and regional champions.
  * *Dispute Note*: bj-league teams were excluded by JBA from 2005 to 2016 (only Chiba Jets participated in 2012).
* **Post-2016 Reform (93rd Edition 2017–18 onward)**: Dropped "総合" to become the *All-Japan Basketball Championship* (全日本バスケットボール選手権大会). Converted into an in-season tournament stretching September through March:
  * 1st & 2nd Rounds: 47 Prefectural and Regional Block champions + B3 teams.
  * 3rd & 4th Rounds: B2 and lower B1 teams enter.
  * Final Round (Quarterfinals, Semifinals, Final): Top B1 seeds clash at major arenas (Saitama Super Arena, Ariake, Yoyogi).

### Era 8: Specialized Competitions & Exhibition Games
* **B.League Early Cup (アーリーカップ, 2017–2019)**: Pre-season regional tournament held in early September across 6 regions (Tohoku, Kanto, Hokushinetsu, Tokai, Kansai, Nishinihon) featuring B1 and B2 teams. Full box scores stored under `event=20` on bleague.jp. Suspended 2020 due to COVID-19.
* **All-Star Games**:
  * **B.League All-Star Game** (2017–present, `event=5`): B.BLACK vs B.WHITE (Yoyogi, Kumamoto, Toyama, Sapporo, Mito, Okinawa, Funabashi).
  * **bj-league All-Star Game** (2007–2016): EAST vs WEST (Ginowan, Niigata, Beppu, Miyagi, Osaka, Saitama Super Arena, Ariake, Akita, Gunma, Sendai).
  * **NBL All-Star Game** (2013, 2015).
* **International / Inter-League In-Season Events**:
  * **bj-KBL Championship Games** (2006–2014): Annual clash between bj-league champions and Korean Basketball League champions.
  * **East Asia Super League (EASL)** (2023–present): Official regional in-season tournament featuring top B.League teams against PBA, KBL, and P.League+ clubs.

### Technical Details of the B.League API & Web Architecture:
* **Schedule Navigation URL Pattern**:
  `https://www.bleague.jp/schedule/?year={YYYY}&mon={MM|all}&event={EVENT_ID}&tab={TAB_ID}`
  * `year`: `2016` through `2026`
  * `tab`: `1` (B1), `2` (B2), `3` (B3)
  * `event`: `2` (B1 Regular Season), `3` (B1 Championship Playoffs), `4` (B1 Relegation Playoff), `5` (All-Star Game), `7` (B2 Regular Season), `8` (B2 Playoffs), `9` (B2 Relegation Playoff), `11` (B1/B2 Promotion/Relegation), `15` (B3 Regular Season), `17` (B2/B3 Promotion/Relegation), `20` (Early Cup).
* **Game Detail URL Pattern**:
  `https://www.bleague.jp/game_detail/?ScheduleKey={KEY}&tab={TAB}`
  * `tab=1`: Club Comparison / Team Stats
  * `tab=2`: Game Recap / Report (試合レポート)
  * `tab=3`: Play-by-Play (テキスト速報)
  * `tab=4`: Full Player & Team Box Score (ボックススコア)
* **RealGM Lineage**:
  * Covers B.League B1 and B2 from 2016–17 to present with complete player season logs, game-by-game team logs, per-game and per-40/per-100 possessions stats, advanced offensive/defensive ratings.

## 4. Standardized Game Log CSV Schema

To harmonize basketball logs across corporate halves (pre-2000), 4-quarter leagues (2000–present), overtime games, neutral-site Emperor's Cup brackets, and All-Star exhibitions, the following **42-column CSV schema** is established:

```csv
game_id,season,era,league_tier,competition_name,game_type,stage_round,game_date,tip_off_time_jst,neutral_site_flag,venue_name,venue_name_ja,city,prefecture,attendance,referee_1,referee_2,referee_3,home_team_id,home_team_name_en,home_team_name_ja,away_team_id,away_team_name_en,away_team_name_ja,home_q1_pts,away_q1_pts,home_q2_pts,away_q2_pts,home_q3_pts,away_q3_pts,home_q4_pts,away_q4_pts,home_ot1_pts,away_ot1_pts,home_ot2_pts,away_ot2_pts,home_final_score,away_final_score,winner,winning_margin,overtime_count,data_source,source_url,notes
```

### Column Specifications:

1. `game_id` *(STRING)*: Unique canonical key (e.g., `BLG_202324_501311`, `BJ_201516_0421`, `NBL_201415_0112`, `JBL_200809_0045`, `JL_198586_0012`, `EMP_202324_0088`).
2. `season` *(STRING)*: Standardized season format `YYYY-YY` (e.g., `2023-24`, `1985-86`).
3. `era` *(STRING)*: Historical era (`Japan_League`, `JBL_Super_League`, `JBL`, `bj_league`, `NBL`, `NBDL`, `B_League`).
4. `league_tier` *(STRING)*: `Tier_1`, `Tier_2`, `Tier_3`, `Domestic_Cup`, `Regional_Cup`, `Exhibition`.
5. `competition_name` *(STRING)*: Official competition (e.g., `B1 League`, `bj-league`, `NBL`, `Emperor's Cup`, `B.League Early Cup`, `All-Star Game`).
6. `game_type` *(STRING)*: `Regular_Season`, `Playoffs`, `Relegation_Playoff`, `Promotion_Relegation`, `In_Season_Cup`, `All_Star`, `Pre_Season`, `Exhibition`.
7. `stage_round` *(STRING)*: Specific match round (e.g., `Round 1`, `Quarterfinals Game 1`, `Championship Final`, `Early Cup Final`, `Round of 16`).
8. `game_date` *(ISO DATE)*: `YYYY-MM-DD`.
9. `tip_off_time_jst` *(STRING)*: Local tip-off time in JST `HH:MM` (e.g., `19:05`).
10. `neutral_site_flag` *(BOOLEAN)*: `TRUE` / `FALSE` (crucial for Emperor's Cup, early bj-league final four, and pre-B.League neutral tours).
11. `venue_name` *(STRING)*: Standardized venue name in English (e.g., `SAGA Arena`, `Ariake Colosseum`, `Yoyogi National Gymnasium 1st`).
12. `venue_name_ja` *(STRING)*: Original venue name in Japanese (e.g., `SAGAアリーナ`, `有明コロシアム`, `代々木第一体育館`).
13. `city` *(STRING)*: Host municipality (e.g., `Saga`, `Tokyo`, `Okinawa`).
14. `prefecture` *(STRING)*: Japanese prefecture (e.g., `Saga`, `Tokyo`, `Okinawa`).
15. `attendance` *(INTEGER)*: Paid attendance (integer count; `null` if unrecorded or behind closed doors).
16. `referee_1` *(STRING)*: Lead referee (Crew Chief).
17. `referee_2` *(STRING)*: Referee 2 (Umpire 1).
18. `referee_3` *(STRING)*: Referee 3 (Umpire 2, for 3-person mechanics).
19. `home_team_id` *(STRING)*: Canonical franchise ID (e.g., `SAGA`, `RYUKYU`, `TOYOTA_ALV`, `TOSHIBA_BRAVE`).
20. `home_team_name_en` *(STRING)*: English name (e.g., `Saga Ballooners`).
21. `home_team_name_ja` *(STRING)*: Japanese name (e.g., `佐賀バルーナーズ`).
22. `away_team_id` *(STRING)*: Canonical away franchise ID.
23. `away_team_name_en` *(STRING)*: English away team name.
24. `away_team_name_ja` *(STRING)*: Japanese away team name.
25. `home_q1_pts` *(INTEGER)*: Home Q1 score (or 1st half score for pre-2000 20-min half games).
26. `away_q1_pts` *(INTEGER)*: Away Q1 score.
27. `home_q2_pts` *(INTEGER)*: Home Q2 score (or 2nd half score for pre-2000 games).
28. `away_q2_pts` *(INTEGER)*: Away Q2 score.
29. `home_q3_pts` *(INTEGER)*: Home Q3 score (`null` for 2-half era).
30. `away_q3_pts` *(INTEGER)*: Away Q3 score.
31. `home_q4_pts` *(INTEGER)*: Home Q4 score (`null` for 2-half era).
32. `away_q4_pts` *(INTEGER)*: Away Q4 score.
33. `home_ot1_pts` *(INTEGER)*: Overtime 1 points home (`null` if regulation).
34. `away_ot1_pts` *(INTEGER)*: Overtime 1 points away.
35. `home_ot2_pts` *(INTEGER)*: Overtime 2 points home (extendable to OT3+ if necessary).
36. `away_ot2_pts` *(INTEGER)*: Overtime 2 points away.
37. `home_final_score` *(INTEGER)*: Total final score home.
38. `away_final_score` *(INTEGER)*: Total final score away.
39. `winner` *(STRING)*: `HOME` or `AWAY`.
40. `winning_margin` *(INTEGER)*: `abs(home_final_score - away_final_score)`.
41. `overtime_count` *(INTEGER)*: Number of extra periods played (`0` = regulation, `1`, `2`, etc.).
42. `data_source` *(STRING)*: Source portal / archive (e.g., `bleague.jp`, `bj-league_archive`, `jba_records`, `realgm`).
43. `source_url` *(STRING)*: Direct source link / permalink.
44. `notes` *(STRING)*: Context notes (e.g., `2011 Earthquake truncated match`, `2020 COVID behind-closed-doors`, `Played under FIBA 2-half rule`).

## 5. Handling Historical Anomalies & Caveats

1. **Pre-2000 FIBA Halves vs Post-2000 Quarters**:
   Prior to FIBA's rule modernization in 2000, Japan League games were played in two 20-minute halves. In the CSV, 1st half points are logged in `home_q1_pts` / `away_q1_pts` and 2nd half points in `home_q2_pts` / `away_q2_pts`, with `q3` and `q4` set to `null` and flagged in `notes`.
2. **2010–11 Great East Japan Earthquake (東北地方太平洋沖地震)**:
   On March 11, 2011, both the JBL and bj-league were severely impacted. JBL terminated the remainder of the 2010–11 season and canceled all playoffs, freezing the standings. In the bj-league, Sendai 89ERS, Saitama Broncos, and Tokyo Apache suspended activities; Sendai and Tokyo forfeited remaining fixtures while Saitama rejoined after disaster recovery. These unplayed games must be recorded as unplayed or omitted from completed game logs with notes in the schedule master.
3. **2019–20 COVID-19 Pandemic Truncation**:
   B.League temporarily held games behind closed doors (recorded with `attendance=0`) before fully suspending and cancelling the regular season on March 27, 2020 after ~40–41 games per club. No B1 Championship was held, and relegation was suspended while top B2 clubs were promoted.
4. **bj-league Mini-Game Playoff Tiebreakers**:
   From 2008 to 2016, bj-league two-legged playoff ties tied 1–1 triggered a separate 10-minute "mini-game" immediately after Game 2. These mini-games should be indexed as independent tiebreaker game records (`stage_round = "Conference SF Tiebreaker Mini-Game"`).
